# Preview semantics

Rules for model, Whole world, mission and animation previews. Engine facts and addresses: [engine-evidence](engine-evidence.md). Source data and serialized records never change through a preview; mark game-dependent approximations explicitly.

## Mission starting scene

- Mission previews show a separate single-player starting scene for the chosen difficulty, built in this order: load-time flagged cleanup and automatic activations, per-instance turret cleanup, AIV vehicle instances and poses, pickup placements, the immediate NEW_GAME_START animation frontier, then the [player tank](#player-tank).
- Load initialization runs every flagged cleanup in entry order before the zero-time events of the ON_STARTUP entries. A launch at either step does not restart an animation the load or the NEW_GAME_START list has started. A range-gated animation (EXECUTION_BY_RANGE) runs only if its attach node is in range of the player's spawn: the spawn of the first vehicle of the chosen AIV layout that the vehicle resource defines.
- Approximations against retail ([animation runtime](engine-evidence.md#animation-runtime)): the game runs `ON_STARTUP` events only in the first frame, by priority, the preview in activation order; the startup frontiers apply the range test once to every started entry, children included, before its first events (no gated entry runs without an AIV spawn; cleanups are not gated); `EXECUTION_BY_ZONE` entries run their zero-time events at start; a clone's nodes come from the prepared scene, where load cleanup already ran (a cleanup outlasting zero time, or a clone bound at nodes changed since load, would differ); gaps between same-flag segments, the morph clamp and event 0x0B (evaluated absolutely, not incrementally) differ.
- Never advance the mission context time or play startup audio to recover a pose (the player tank's mode change runs on its own clock: [player tank](#player-tank)). Background actors stay at their starting layout; AI, combat, inventory, terrain settling, saved-game state and mission progression are not simulated.
- Unplaced scripted actors stay hidden in world context, with a diagnostic, until placed; explicit individual inspection may use a clearly labeled stored pose. No generic offset on static terrain or ordinary zero transforms.
- Names never identify instances; cloned vehicles keep their source-node provenance. Shared world animation bindings share state and checkpoint aliases; copied animation roots and effects keep separate identities.
- Edits invalidate the frozen preview context and rebuild the baseline.
- Source World child rows expose initialized hidden branches. Their eyes use the shared part-visibility overrides before scene traversal, so enabling destroyed geometry prepares its meshes rather than merely clearing a hide mask. Overrides never modify source flags, simulation state or history; a parent gate, pending placement and LOD still constrain traversal. Exact tree selection is retained separately from whole-object hit ownership through inspection refresh and animation attachment changes.

## Player tank

- The local player's tank is the first vehicle the chosen AIV layout spawns (its placement record, never the name `bft_00`). Every mission preview draws it as the game shows it settled after a fresh start in a state: master type, primary and secondary weapon ([vehicles](engine-evidence.md#vehicles)). Without a choice it draws the mission's start, which the data decides: tracked, the first listed weapon the mission allows as secondary and the second as primary (HE Mortar and RFPG in every shipped mission; the single-player threshold rule, with the folder number standing in for the mission on m7–m13); compiled worlds and animation previews always draw that. A source map's view takes a choice for the open map (the tank's Properties card, MCP `scene_options` `bftType`/`bftPrimary`/`bftSecondary`): every opening starts at the mission's start; rebuilds, undo, save, reload and difficulty keep it; it is never saved and makes no edit, draft or undo step. What the view draws, when not the start, is in the context's labels (`MissionLayoutSelection.PlayerTank`); a change rebuilds the view as a difficulty change does, after a pending edit is applied or discarded.
- Weapons: every listed weapon's model under `gun` is switched off, then the primary pair and the secondary's model on; a single-mount secondary rests at its stored position at scale 1 with the turret doors collapsed. Approximations: the loaded mine, missile or nuke model is not hung on the launcher, the primary's scroll is not animated, and ownership and ammo are not simulated (every weapon can be chosen).
- Master type: the end of the vehicle resource's mode change from track (sub: amphibious first), run after the NEW_GAME_START frontier on the tank only, its entries in a private player with its own work allowance ticking at 60 Hz until they finish (at most 10 s per list), with no mission clock, audio, effect or other actor; a flag-0x20 entry runs no cleanup first, since the load stopped it. Its flags and poses are published only when every list finished and all its writes were admitted, otherwise the tank stays tracked and the reason goes to Problems; a tank that cannot be prepared at all is reported as such, not as missing. A part the change leaves shown in a morphed shape draws a morphed copy of its model, which shares its model's polygons, so polygon zone edits stay incremental while such a part is shown. Not simulated: the code's height changes (sub dive, floating), trails, wakes and bubbles, the props' speed and the hover shadow's clearance test.
- A type is offered when the tank's track block names its list and the map's animations hold every entry it starts; one an edit or a difficulty change removes returns to Tracked, and a missing tank keeps the choice for when one spawns. In sub a forbidden secondary is replaced by the weapon the game switches to (every weapon owned and loaded, another bank on its first side).
- An opening that hides the tank (the m2, m4 and m5 drop-offs) keeps it hidden, whatever the choice.

## Difficulty

- Easy/Medium/Hard is one shared, saved app preference, default Medium; opening a source project restores the difficulty its `.zstudio/settings.json` remembers (a project seen for the first time remembers the current one). It stays enabled when Map is hidden because animation bindings use the mission context.
- Easy selects `aiv_easy.zrd` and `vehicle_easy.zrd`, Medium `aiv.zrd` and `vehicle.zrd`, Hard `aiv_hard.zrd` and `vehicle_hard.zrd`; each absent variant falls back independently to its default file. Empty valid variants stay empty; a malformed selected resource reports an error and keeps the last valid preview. Never merge layouts or infer difficulty from actor name suffixes.
- Requested difficulty and effective resources are part of the context/cache identity and the labels. Authored AIV variant selection is a preview capability, not engine parity ([resource archives](engine-evidence.md#resource-archives)).
- Changing difficulty keeps the playhead and playback intent, free or followed camera, grid and height, LOD, custom range and authored selection; a followed camera is reapplied at the reconstructed playhead. Bindings and inspection remap only through unambiguous actor identity and source provenance, never old clone indices; absent or ambiguous instances clear the binding. Audio resources and the output stay warm. Superseded requests never publish; a request's cancellation token never becomes the lifetime of a successfully loaded animated viewport. No edit or undo entry is created.

## Turrets

- Turrets initialize from `ai.zrd` for each matching scene instance, with its pattern and destruction-animation rules ([`ai.zrd`](engine-evidence.md#aizrd)).
- Apply the selected primary cleanup to the actual instance, with reset components scoped to that turret; keep destroyed geometry for later animation playback. Hide initialization-only fire-point and effect helpers without deleting source records.

## Pickups

- Pickups use the engine's 40-type catalog and `puNNN` model templates. Select `puppies_easy.zrd`/`puppies.zrd`/`puppies_hard.zrd` independently, falling back to the default only when the variant is absent. These resources are ordered placement lists, not name/value pairs.
- Keep XYZ, radian Euler rotation, template scale, amount/default amount and respawn metadata; hide `bvol` collision geometry. A copy without a `bvol` is destroyed as the game destroys it, and the next placement takes its name. Approximation: the preview looks for `bvol` among the copy's direct children, the game's `FindSubNodeByName` searches the whole subtree ([pickups](engine-evidence.md#pickups)); every shipped pickup keeps it as a direct child. Show every authored pickup regardless of player inventory.
- Bind clones by archive/resource/record identity and template provenance; different pickup resources never remap by generated name or clone index. Invalid individual placements get resource/record diagnostics without discarding valid siblings.
- Never simulate collection, respawn or terrain snapping to invent a starting placement.

## LOD

- LOD 0 is the nearest, highest-detail authored band of each parent group, not one global map distance; higher ranks clamp independently per object.
- Animation LOD selection changes only visible poses and keeps inactive variants' simulation state.
- Model previews and exports use the enclosing LOD group where present. Map, model and animation pickers behave alike.

## Materials and texture cycles

- Texture cycles follow the runtime creation order ([textures](engine-evidence.md#textures-and-the-rendering-target)); keep effect-setup material bindings separate from serialized GameZ data. Preload cycle maps; cycle sampling is deterministic when seeking.
- Transparent textures without an alpha plane key decoded RGB565 zero when flag 2 is set ([colour keys](engine-evidence.md#textures-and-the-rendering-target)).
- Alpha blending keeps opaque depth writes but writes no depth for transparent surfaces, including alpha-zero texels. Transparent surfaces draw through the same explicit mesh input layout as opaque ones.
- Opacity follows the SetLitFlag-named inherited alpha override ([animation runtime](engine-evidence.md#animation-runtime)); never render stored opacity unconditionally.

## Horizon

- The horizon follows the active viewport camera: XYZ for the Player horizon fallback, or an explicit XZ-only binding. Render it before scene geometry without depth writes or scene near/far clipping; exclude it from framing and depth bounds.
- Animation world poses obey the Horizon toggle; it follows the camera while paused and while Follow camera is active. Whole world keeps its own Horizon checkbox.

## Cameras

- Finalize preview cameras after the per-frame inertia and navigation updates and before the frame's camera matrices are captured. Camera notifications only invalidate; they never recursively reapply animation poses.
- Frame pipeline, depth bounds, clipping and the navigation floor: [architecture](architecture.md#rendering-pipeline).
- Free orbit, Fly and authored preview cameras stay upright around +Y with ±89° pitch, keeping the orbit target or the Fly/authored position. Named top/bottom views are exactly axis-aligned, with a stable basis when free orbit leaves the pole.
- No built-in keyboard camera control (owner decision 2026-10-22): with the 3D view focused, arrows and Page Up/Down are consumed without moving the camera (focus stays in the view) and W/A/S/D/Z/Q, Backspace and the mouse's Back/Forward buttons do not move the camera (outside Fly); only a left click navigates with the view cube; touch acts as the mouse (a tap is a left click, press-and-hold a right click); the wheel does nothing during an AI path transform, a drop-window move or a box selection; the keys are Home and the numpad shortcuts ([3D navigation](../3d-navigation.md)) and Fly's.
- Shared Blender-style navigation runs before any picking of the press: middle orbit, Shift+middle pan, Ctrl+middle pointer-directed zoom (orthographic pointer anchoring), Ctrl+Shift+middle dolly, with decaying inertia. Resolve held Shift/Ctrl throughout a middle drag regardless of press order; defer pivot picking until orbit movement begins; clear the previous mode's inertia on transitions.
- The orbit pivot is the nearest eligible rendered triangle under the first unmodified middle press (transformed instances and runtime animation meshes included; helpers and horizons excluded); orbit rotates eye offset and basis together about that world-space pivot without recentering. Zoom travel speed comes from the camera-to-hit distance along the pointer ray, refreshed between substeps of at most one wheel step and during inertia, keeping the last reference on a miss. Surface queries use the current camera without mutating the pivot or depending on cached clipping planes.
- Projection changes keep the target plane's apparent scale. Clipping uses perspective side planes or parallel orthographic planes; picking, framing, billboards, horizon and constant-pixel pickup handles support both. Frame uses visible transformed geometry with stable source-node/descendant selection, excluding horizon, grid and handles.
- Fly: [workbench](workbench.md#fly-camera); its motion integrates before camera finalization. Terrain collision is not simulated for any camera.
- Axis views enter orthographic and return to perspective on orbit; an explicitly chosen orthographic projection persists. Camera snapshots keep projection, width and axis state through refreshes. Manual navigation exits Follow camera without changing playback or serialized data.
- Camera animation channels follow the engine's camera semantics ([cameras](engine-evidence.md#animation-runtime)): never treat Euler angles as eye coordinates or translation as a look-at target; the radian horizontal FOV converts to degrees at the preview-frame boundary. Camera keyframes keep parent transforms and deterministic seeking. Follow camera toggles apply immediately while paused.
- Idle regression captures read the presented back buffer without forcing a render or resize.

## Ground grid, collision and height

- In isolated previews Grid and Ground collision are independent controls, both on by default; Core callers opt into `GroundPlaneEnabled`. Source-map previews use an immutable baseline spatial index with a runtime geometry overlay and origin-based contact; flat-plane controls do not apply.
- Grid visibility changes only rendering. The grid shader is independent of the scene far distance, antialiased, depth-tested without depth writes; only its pass disables depth clipping and clamps output depth. The grid never takes part in picking, framing or near/far bounds.
- Isolated collision uses a world Y=0 plane with mesh support. Source-map collision queries beneath the moving node origin, excluding its bound assembly, using current active standable geometry, world transforms and morphs at fixed nearest LOD. Viewport eyes and camera zones do not change collision. Runtime-owned surfaces suppress their baseline triangles, including when hidden. Descending contact follows the recorded engine displacement, damping, release and impact rules; subsequent authored sinking is allowed. Collision geometry is derived and refreshed after rewind/reset. Camera and general rigid-body collision are not simulated. Preload collision samples; keep cue gain on seek and resume.
- GUI playback and source path analysis opt into procedural translation limits at world Y −500 and 500 (including Height); Core mission initialization does not. First crossing clamps the origin and completes that motion without impact cues; other sequences continue and later events may reposition/relaunch. Cameras and authored keyframes are exempt. Duration, seek, playback and paths share the same settings. Stationary path tails are omitted. Camera guides are orange (#F5A742), objects teal (#6FC8D6), with alpha-only selection emphasis.
- Height defaults to 0, accepts −999 to 999 game units and is preview-only; negative values lower the animation; grid and collision plane stay at Y=0. Event and simulation coordinates stay authored: apply the world-Y offset once to output poses, effects, lights, cameras and audio and to ground-support measurements. Never translate serialized records or the static map.
- In isolated previews Grid, collision and Height changes rebuild at the current playhead, keep playback and a custom range, measure the Auto range again in the background and invalidate checkpoints. Source-world scenario changes use the stopped/reset contract below.

## Source-world animation session

- The map's scenario is one immutable `AnimationScenario` per map document (`AnimationScenarioSession`, with a change event): starts, included call paths, condition overrides on IF and ELSEIF, inputs (player at start/custom/none, seed, effects detail, phase, height, beam origin) and rules; the session, analyses, drop windows and MCP all read it. It is saved per map in `.zstudio/settings.json` (`AnimationScenarioRecord`: bindings as definition file, ordinal, listing occurrence and root; events as definition and source record; call paths as a tree of call event and target binding; a map's entry at most 64 KB, dropping the deepest included paths with a notice beyond it; the defaults remove the entry) and restored when the map is shown; a recompile remaps it through source provenance, never names. A start, included call path or condition switch that no longer matches is kept and marked (design §12; `AnimationScenarioSession.Marked`, the record's marked part with anchors to the starts its items hang off): never run, saved with the scenario, resolved again with every later compilation (so undo and redo bring it back), and listed per kind in the Scenario tab (Remove, Keep) and as the scenario's own Problems rows, which a source build's report never replaces; Keep stops asking until more are marked (saved with the scenario, `keptMarks`; a restore whose files mark more of a kind asks again), Remove drops them, Reset clears them. A marked start comes back at its place among the starts; an exclusion takes the marked included paths at or below it (under a stand-in the marked primary's same calls too, also where a call names its target by pattern, and for a call into the stand-in's binding the same call into the marked primary's), Exclude all every one, and This animation only the other starts' (it excludes their own paths) (`AnimationScenarioSession.Exclude`). Each marked item's words name its start or the call it sits in and the sequence (names from the current compilation where it has them), numbered when two would read the same. When a recompile takes the primary's own binding away, the GUI's rebase, and opening a binding of the marked primary's definition (`StandsInFor`: Open, Return, `animation_mode open`, also on files edited while the map was closed), make it the primary only as a stand-in (`AnimationScenarioSession.StandIn`, saved `standIn`; the binding picker hands the role to the binding it chooses): at the marked primary's time; opening another definition leaves it an ordinary start that still stands in (the two share one identity: the primary's, then that start's), and opening its binding again makes it the primary stand-in again; when the marked primary matches again (an undo) it takes the stand-in's place back and the stand-in goes, never marked. What the user did while it stood in is quiet then (owner decision): the stand-in's time, call paths and switches move to the original (its switch replaces the original's marked one; a call that names its target by pattern, as m1 `rocket1.zad`'s `call_rocket1*`, reaches the original's own sibling binding of that definition, `call_rocket11` for `call_rocket12`, and what lies below it follows), the stand-in's binding counts as the original's for the calls into it and the starts of it, and what still cannot apply is dropped without a mark or notice; what the edit itself marked keeps its marks. A stand-in the scenario no longer has (removed, or the binding picker choosing a start already in it) takes the marked original with it, unlisted. **Remove** of the starts and Reset keep the original only hidden (saved `originalRemoved`: not counted, listed or run), as the place the stand-in hands back to when an undo takes the stand-in's own binding away; while that binding still matches, the original matching again is let go and the stand-in stays the start; when both no longer match, the stand-in folds into the original, which shows marked again. Nothing resets it silently: opening a binding in Animation mode makes it the primary: another definition keeps the previous primary as an ordinary start (`WithPrimary(entry, keepPrevious: true)`; refused at 32 starts), another binding of the primary's definition replaces it at its time (`keepPrevious: false`), and Exit, difficulty, LOD, edits, rebuilds and other maps keep it; only Reset (the opened binding stays primary) clears it.
- A preview schedule contains up to 32 exact activations on the shared 0–3,600-second clock. The primary has empty GUID and empty lineage prefix; additional activations use `/@<guid:N>` prefixes. Starts are admitted at the first 60 Hz boundary at or after their requested time, in list order for ties. Source thresholds stay in their own clocks; Timing's marks use the shared clock. Completed shared world poses persist under authored cleanup rules. Future starts participate in completion, duration, paths, audio preparation and checkpoints. Include and condition policies remain finite across the whole scenario. Schedule edits stop/detach, never edit source files; revision changes invalidate exact bindings. Fit range to starts uses the latest start plus 30 seconds, capped at 3,600, without duration analysis. The transport steps one 1/60 s tick and reports seconds only.

- Source maps attach complete evaluated-subset frames to their existing initialized world. They never open the compiled animation viewer, replace the world, duplicate LOD/Difficulty controls or reframe on preparation. Shared replacements suppress only their matching static placements; missing overrides return to baseline and expired instance visuals disappear. Detach restores current static rendering, picking and overlays. Runtime-owned objects are inspection-only.
- Selection/expansion is structural and bounded: at most 100 direct references per page and 4,096 retained dependency-path records per session. It does no transitive closure, player construction, duration/path evaluation or audio decoding. Exact source addresses and compiled GUIDs are revision-scoped; recompilation must not remap by name. Nonserialized compiler provenance links emitted events to shared source-edit records without changing archive bytes or SI arithmetic.
- The run's target is Animation mode's opened binding; there is no candidate or pinned target, and selection only inspects. Entering, switching the opened definition and Exit detach the run (a new preview id) without starting, evaluating or moving the camera. Stop restores the static baseline and zero playhead and keeps the scenario; Exit also leaves the mode. First source-draft entry detaches and marks old evidence stale. Apply, Revert and undo/redo never resume the old run: a map edit (Apply, undo, redo) ends it, so the playhead returns to 0 s. A save changes only the document's revision, not its built map or the project's content: the run (playing or not), its playhead, the graph's and Timing's selection, the playback range with its Auto measurement, the paths and the drop windows stay, and the card's source model stays current (`WorldAnimationTarget` holds the built map and no revision; reads of the sources key on `DocumentModel.SourceState`, the built map and the project's content revision); MCP reports the new revision and revision-guarded requests refuse the old one. A scenario or inclusion change stops playback and re-evaluates silently up to the playhead it had (as a seek); a change made while another re-evaluates keeps that re-evaluation's time, an MCP call waits only for its own re-evaluation, and a successful one clears the transport's failure line. A GUI action refused for a pending draft leaves that line as soon as no draft of any kind remains (`RefreshWorldAnimationRun`, which every draft's end refreshes). LOD/Difficulty changes use the normal world-update pipeline and a new baseline, stopped at zero; the mode follows its definition onto the rebuilt map.
- Manual sessions admit only the root initially. Include is per resolved callsite path, distinct from expansion and guide visibility; ancestors must be included explicitly. The same immutable finite policy gates type 19/24 launch, path analysis and audio collection, including dormant branches and cleanup. Excluded non-waiting calls advance with an omission; excluded waited calls block that sequence while other sequences continue. Under every policy a call whose path would pass 8,192 characters (a call cycle that every-call admission follows) is excluded as well, its trace status "Call path too deep" (Timing's and the Log's result `excluded · call path too deep`, the overlay row's detail, a Preview notice); no child path is ever empty, so no child runs with a start's own path. Included unresolved calls preserve unavailable/advance behavior and incomplete coverage. Duplicate starts neither join nor wait on an existing instance. No automatic child admission, including for `start_north`.
- The player reference at the playhead (`AnimationFrame.PlayerReference`: position, source aiv start, opening handover, custom, none or callback 10/17, frozen after callback 11; every change in `PlayerReferences`) is what range conditions and range gates measure from; the 3D view's range tests and MCP `animation_runtime player` show it (retail m2 opening: aiv start frozen at 0 s, callback 10 at 24.967 s). What they measure to is the callback node: each frame lists it for every instance whose binding tests a range (`AnimationFrame.Attaches`: a range gate or a PLAYER_RANGE IF/ELSEIF; its run pose, also for a node without a model, which has no pose in `Nodes`), and each PLAYER_RANGE result keeps where it measured from (`AnimationConditionResult.Center`, authored space).
- The scenario's rules (`AnimationExecutionPolicy.Rules`, immutable, with these defaults) decide what the game would: **Player** at start (the mission's `PlayerStart`; callback 10 or 17 moves it to the player tank root's world position at that event, 11 freezes it; a run without the mission's opening that sets the player's pose starts from the opening's handover, its cleanup's callback 10) or a custom point or none; **Prerequisites** ignored for the scenario's own starts and checked on every start the run makes (or checked everywhere, or ignored); **Range gates** as the game (or ignored); **Zone gates** ignored (or the caller's view zone set; none yet means never runs). Refused starts are reported with their reason, never silently dropped. A scheduled start bypassing unmet prerequisites adds a Support note. Every start attempt that checks prerequisites is recorded as evidence (`AnimationPrerequisiteStart`: met, refused or ignored by the rule, with each check), bounded like the refusals; it changes nothing in the run. Waiting sequences still need authored releases. No external hit/startup/release simulator is implied. Conditions are scoped to exact call paths/events, on IF and ELSEIF alike. Phase defaults to Runtime and Height to 0; inspecting cleanup does not change phase. Changes reset incompatible state without autoplay.
- The Inspector reads what the run recorded and never recomputes its semantics: a Condition card's rule in words uses the latest pass's evidence (`AnimationConditionResult`: the measured distance from the player reference to the attach node, the seeded draw and its value, the effects level, the cover height or why it was not evaluable) and, before a pass, the scenario's inputs (the player at start, custom or none against the attach node's position; the seed; the effects detail); a forced switch is stated as forced. Prerequisite marks are the binding's latest start attempt's checks (`AnimationPrerequisiteStart`), else an animation item's state at the playhead (met unless that binding is stopped). Notices (design §15) come from the frame's preview diagnostics of the shown bindings (by entry, sequence and event), its refusals and prerequisite-ignored starts, its excluded calls that would change the run (below), the graph's sequence diagnostics and unavailable calls, and the trace, condition and diagnostics limits; each is counted on its graph node and Timing lane. The player keeps at most 2,000 distinct diagnostics (`AnimationPlayer.MaximumDiagnosticGroups`), grouped by entry, sequence and event, never by instance (a call cycle starts instances without end), a generic support or approximation note (`AnimationPlayer.IsGenericNote`) once per entry; a call left out because its path is too deep is always kept; what the limit leaves out is counted in one note (entry −1), shown as a notice and, in a compiled preview, a Diagnostics row. A compiled preview's strip lists errors and warnings first and at most 500 rows, then how many more `animation_runtime problems` lists. The Scenario tab's Prerequisites count is the run's refused and ignored start attempts.
- Excluded calls that would change the run (design §15; owner decision 2026-10-29: calls stay excluded by default, and these are flagged so the preview never silently differs from the game; lead decision 2026-10-29: a what-if run decides it). The player records the first dispatch of every excluded call path it reaches (`AnimationFrame.ExcludedCalls`: path, caller, call event, target and time; at most 4,096, then `ExcludedCallsTruncated`; evidence only, checkpointed with the run, none while measuring the duration). A static prefilter (`AnimationExcludedEffects.Candidates`, one per graph index, its walks cached per called binding) picks the candidates in dispatch order: a call whose target, or anything it can call or reset in turn (every sequence, the cleanup and RESET_STATE included, every branch, whatever the prerequisites; at most 4,096 bindings, each once, past which the call is a candidate), has a `STOP_ANIMATION`, `RESET_ANIMATION` or `INVALIDATE_ANIMATION` whose name resolves, as the player resolves it (the first entry of the name, its base record, never entry 0; calls: a positive entry cache, else the first entry of the name), to a binding that can be part of the base run: a scenario start or the target of an included call path (any binding under every-call admission). It may over-select, never miss. Each candidate is then checked by a what-if run (`AnimationPlayer.RunWhatIf`: a separate player with the same context, starts, inputs, rules and condition switches and the policy `AnimationExecutionPolicy.WithSubtree`, which also includes that path and every path below it; under every-call admission the exclusions at or below it go), from 0 s to the playback range's end (Auto or custom) or until that run ends or only waits on excluded calls; it keeps no checkpoints. It records the first stop, reset or invalidate by name that an instance of the subtree (or a cleanup a reset of the subtree started) applies, as the player applies it, to a binding of the base run, a scenario start or a binding a call outside the subtree started, acting on the base run's own instance: a stop of a running binding, a reset of one not stopped or unavailable, an invalidate of one not invalidated yet (an invalidate of a binding the base run has not started counts once a start outside the subtree is refused for it). An effect the base run applies itself is not one: a baseline run (`AnimationPlayer.RunBaseline`, the scenario as it is to the same end, once per scenario and range; at most 65,536) records every by-name event the base run applies, and the same event on the same binding at the same time in the what-if is skipped (the subtree's call started an animation a moment before the base run's own call of it, which was then refused as already running: retail m6 and m13 `reset_the_transporters`). Its time is that event's dispatch on the shared clock (where Timing ends what it stopped), the event is named as Timing names a stop (`train_fall_trigger › placeholder › Stop animation destroy_tr_bridge`), and it lists the call paths at or below the excluded one it ran (at most 4,096). Nothing applied, no notice: a refused nested call, a false branch, an animation that stopped itself first or an effect on what the subtree started itself flags nothing. The GUI (`AnimationWhatIfChecks`, the source run's and the compiled preview's) runs the baseline and then the first 8 candidates in dispatch order, one after another, off the UI thread on a snapshot of the run's frozen context, caches each result per run (context and player settings), binding, scenario value and range end, cancels them on a scenario change, an edit, a rebuild, Exit or the compiled preview's end, and waits while the range is measured or the compiled preview loads or seeks; they never touch the visible run, the playhead or the 3D view. Its notice (kind `excludedEffect`, one per call path) reads "train_fall_trigger is excluded: in the game it stops destroy_tr_bridge at 23.017 s" and sits on the call's row and sequence node, the call's port and reference node and the affected binding's node and Timing group, where a dashed marker shows that time; its Include includes exactly what the what-if ran (`IncludePaths`: the call's path and the paths below it it ran; in a compiled preview the path and the exclusions below it), and the run re-evaluates. While checks run, and for candidates past the first 8 or a what-if that could not reach the range's end (a preview allowance), one `excludedCheck` notice says so ("Checking whether 1 excluded call changes the run in the game…", "3 more excluded calls not checked: …") and the chip reads "n · checking…".
- Timing (`AnimationTiming`, Core) projects one frame: the scenario's starts (each a top-level group, refused and prerequisite-ignored starts marked from `Refusals` and `PrerequisiteStarts`), the instances their calls reached (parent from `AnimationInstanceStatus.ParentInstance` or the launching trace's `ChildInstance`; grouped by definition as `AnimationGraphIndex.Group` groups them, so a wildcard's bindings and repeated calls share lanes; a call into a definition already on its caller chain joins that ancestor's group, as Structure draws a cycle as one back-link, so nesting is at most as deep as the chain's distinct definitions; a called group's key is a hash of its caller group's key and its definition, short and stable from frame to frame), one lane per sequence (`AllSequences` order, Cleanup first). Marks come from the retained trace (dispositions excluded, excluded_wait, duplicate, refused, unresolved; IF/ELSEIF with their `AnimationConditionResult` by occurrence and event, an ELSEIF decided inside its IF from the result alone; CALL_SEQUENCE with the lane it released from the phase recorded in its own tick, else "nothing waited", or "not known" where that time's phases were discarded; LOOP; ELSE and ENDIF only structure), skipped spans (at the deciding marker's occurrence) and refused scenario starts; conditions and skips older than the retained trace go with it. Only an event still current at the frame is running; one a stop cut short is an interval (or an instant) ending at the stop, its result saying why (`until 2.350 s · stopped by piece3_hit_water › Stop sequence move_piece3`, `stopped with its animation by …`, `reset with its animation by …`, `abandoned: its animation started again`, `unavailable`; `AnimationTimingMark.Stopped`). Bands come from `AnimationFrame.Phases` (`AnimationSequencePhase`: every sequence state change at tick resolution, the released-by event, the gate that pauses it, stops and each instance's end with its start; at most 20,000 retained, the oldest 10,000 dropped and counted in `PhasesDropped`): waiting for release (released by the next phase's releaser; "no releaser left" by the state rule below), waiting for threshold (ending at its event's earliest occurrence since it began; "→ due" only at that occurrence or, while it still waits at that event, from the clock at the frame; a wait begun before the retained trace only where the next phase or the status keeps the sequence at that event on that pass), paused, blocked, stopped, complete; a band is open only while its instance lives; an instance's consecutive bands stay apart (a release separates them), and several instances' overlapping bands of one kind are one under the longest one's label. Thresholds are never a schedule: a bracket needs its observed anchor (the instance's start for the animation clock, as its status, its Finished phase or its launch records it, never its first occurrence; the latest occurrence of the sequence's first event for the sequence clock; the previous occurrence's completion for after previous or, for a first event, the start of its threshold wait), and a later occurrence names the phase that held it where one did. Missing START_TIME means ANIMATION_OFFSET 0, not a measured time. Truncation never proves an event did not execute. Everything is in seconds (`AnimationSeconds`); a mark's seek goes to the end of the tick that ran it; drags snap to 0.025 s (Shift 0.1 s, Ctrl 0.001 s). The playback range is Auto (the duration analysis below) until it is lengthened or edited; the visible range is independent; runtime seek/range is limited to 0–3,600 s and a seek shows the 60 Hz tick at or before it. The analysis is bounded and never mutates the visible run.
- A shown sequence's state is one Core rule (`AnimationSequenceStates`) the Timing lanes and the graph's run overlay share, over the same instances (those the player holds and those it released that left evidence): a live instance's sequence is in its status at the frame (paused while a gate holds it running or waiting for its threshold, stopped during its binding's stop delay or once a cleanup replaced it); an ended instance's is what it reached: complete only if it completed (its last phase, its held status, or its final event completed, never one a stop cut short), unavailable if it was, otherwise stopped, since an instance ends naturally only with every sequence complete, waiting for release or unavailable, and a stop or reset freezes the rest ([animation runtime](engine-evidence.md#animation-runtime)); with neither, not started. Of several instances the latest live one decides, else the one whose evidence ends last. A live instance's waiting sequence has no releaser left when every CALL_SEQUENCE and contact-releasing motion of its instance that targets it (the graph's Release and ContactRelease edges, a cleanup's aside) is past and its sequence does not loop.
- Show guides only toggles compatible cached paths. Without a match, offer Evaluate paths. Explicit path evaluation uses separate `AnimationPlayer.AnalyzePaths` state with the current root, baseline, phase, height, scenario and inclusion; it is silent and preserves frame/playhead, playback/audio, camera and the panel's selection. It also records where procedural motion touched the ground and where a contact played its impact sound (`AnimationPaths.Markers`, at most 4,096; a timeout without a floor is no contact). A guide's position at the playhead interpolates its samples (none before its first; its last after the end; a discontinuity jumps at its own sample). Cache compatibility includes the built map (a save keeps it), exact binding, baseline (the world's, renewed when it is rebuilt or replaced), scenario (its value, so Exit and reopening, or a change undone, keep cached paths showable) and purpose/coverage. Existing limits remain: 128 tracks, 216,001 samples and 15 seconds per analysis; 32 cached sets and 432,002 aggregate points. Report partial, blocked, limited, canceled and stale results explicitly.
- Dedicated VTOL analysis uses a separate named recipe/scenario that admits only recognized flight-to-callback-911 callsites, with a visible callsite list and disclosure. It is offered only for a definition with a recognised flight; in a source map it belongs to Animation mode on that definition. Its callback's conditions follow the map's scenario's condition switches (owner decision: overrides on the flight as primary start, each window's call path its own), and its rules, the player among them, else the game rules with the player at start; nothing is forced true (with the player at start, retail m4 flufvtol_flying releases nothing; its five paths forced True release 25). The shown analysis records the scenario it used: a change to its player, switches or rules marks it stale (analyze and moves refused) until it is shown again, and publication rechecks it. A compiled Whole world shows it read only in its Drop windows chip and takes its player from MCP `animation_scenario inputs {player}`. It never changes main inclusion or publishes main-run frames. It requires the existing complete-flight-cycle proof; incomplete/limited results have diagnostics and no editable windows. Window movement always uses `PlanMove` → `PreviewMove` → `SourceVtolDropEdits`: only the CALL SEQUENCE_OFFSET start scalar changes; STOP EVENT_OFFSET duration, neighbors and other semantics are preserved. No resize grip or inferred endpoint. The Inspector's event fields stay independently editable through `SourceAnimationEdits`, with the warning that editing a recognized field can change/remove its drop-window relationship. Generic draft entry stales prior drop analysis; fresh analysis is explicit.
- Analysis and seek are silent. Pause/Stop/Exit silence output; resume reconciles still-live cues at their logical offsets. Browsing prepares no output and excluded audio is not decoded. One Follow camera toggle starts off; disabling restores the free camera, direct navigation disengages from the displayed view, and Stop/Exit remove the override. Navigation and guide visibility do not change simulation.
- Publications recheck the built world (a save keeps it), source/dependency revision, baseline, session, scenario, run and operation generation immediately before application. Seek supersedes old seeks while retaining the last complete frame. Stop, retarget, draft entry and Exit immediately close publication; stale workers release only their own resources. Fullscreen, hide-UI and theme changes retain the same viewport/session and drafts. GUI and MCP share these commands, identities, budgets, source transactions and refusal reasons.

## Animation audio

- Audio is unmuted by default; volume starts at 50 %. A device failure shows a diagnostic and checks Mute (unchecking retries asynchronously); an unavailable sample is reported without disabling other sounds. Nonspatial approximation: no original attenuation, pitch or mixing.
- Prepare audio from a frozen dependency snapshot, including admitted child animations, branches, cleanup and procedural impact samples (an isolated preview's every-call policy traverses as without a policy, except that while an excluded path lies below a call path its children are admitted by path as `Includes` admits them, so excluded calls decode nothing). Decode only reachable samples off the UI thread; reuse one warm shared-mode WASAPI output (50 ms requested buffer, up to 32 voices) through pause, mute and stop. Sound cues, pause, mute and seek never initialize or dispose native audio devices on the UI thread. Scrubbing is silent; Play after seeking resumes still-active sounds without replaying finished ones.
- Keep active one-shot sounds in seek snapshots until their WAV duration ends, so mute and pause/resume do not drop them. Keep independent voice cursors and generation checks across cancellation, seek and asynchronous shutdown. Closing the preview cancels preparation and releases its output asynchronously.

## Animation structure

- `AnimationGraph` (over a map-wide `AnimationGraphIndex` of callers, needed-by and definition groups) is the one model of an opened binding the Structure view, MCP and the compiled viewer read: deterministic columns (0 activation and the bindings that call it without being called back, 1 its frame, then per call depth a reference column and a column of expanded frames), cleanup then runtime sequences in source order, grouped rows (one series, one target definition, at most 100 members), condition blocks with every branch labelled (an empty branch reads `(nothing)`, a missing ELSE is an implicit empty `if false`) and loop brackets from event 0, and edges bundled per row and target. Calls and by-name events resolve as the runtime does (positive entry cache, else the first entry of the name; by-name never entry 0); a call to a binding on its own call path is a back-link to that binding's activation. Pages hold at most 100 rows; at most 64 expanded groups, each one frame showing its chosen member (`WithMember`; the first by default), whose reference node draws no relations of its own. `AnimationRunOverlay` projects a frame onto it (binding and row states: a row whose latest occurrence a stop cut short is `Stopped`, its last end the stop; sequence states and `no releaser left` by the rule Timing shares (`AnimationSequenceStates`), releasers, a blocked sequence's excluded call and its edge, each binding's latest prerequisite start, condition passes up to 40, reference nodes, edges) from recorded evidence only. `AnimationSeconds` formats three-decimal seconds and snaps drags to 0.025 s (0.1 s, 0.001 s); the step is one 1/60 s tick.
- `AnimationGraphLayout` is the graph's deterministic geometry (DIP at 100 %): the activation column (232 wide; calling bindings under it), each frame filled column by column (232-wide nodes, 24 gutters, a column as tall as its tallest node or 340), reference columns 48 to the right, expanded frames beside their reference node. Every band has a fixed height (header 28, state line 18, row 22, question 26, outcome 24, pass strip 16, branch 20, closing line 6; an expanded group's frame header 52 with its members' line), so layout needs no text measurement. Edges are routed orthogonally through gutters, a frame's bottom channel, the outer channel above everything (edges into an activation: back-links, by-name events naming an ancestor) and the channel below everything (prerequisites, far columns), lanes 4 apart, edges into one target sharing their last corridor lane (one trunk); every gutter, frame padding, corridor and the margin left of the activation column is as wide as its lanes need, so edges with different ends never share a line (an edge entering a header level with another edge's line enters 5 or 9 DIP above or below its middle); a by-name event naming its own binding ends on its frame's header. No segment crosses a node; a label is drawn only where it stays off every node and every other label. Collapsed sequences and rows beyond the first twelve send their edges from the header or the Show all line. A question in a loop always has a pass strip; another gets one once it shows more passes than its outcome line holds (3), kept for the session as presentation. The layout follows presentation only (collapsed, opened, Show all, listed members, pass strips); it never includes, runs or evaluates anything.

## Animation runtime and duration

- Runtime simulation state stays separate from serialized data. The primary sequence is cleanup, not an extra runtime sequence. Events, procedural launch and gravity follow [animation runtime](engine-evidence.md#animation-runtime) (0x16 releases, 0x17 stops; launch bytes 32/40/48/56 are yaw, pitch, speed, radial acceleration).
- Bindings keep the engine's entry states (load cleanup leaves flag-0x20 entries stopped); a start while the base record runs elsewhere is a clone, and by-name stop, reset and invalidate act on the first entry of the name's base record only. Every reset restores the rest pose and runs the cleanup's zero-time events at once; a restarted flag-0x20 entry cleans up first without the restore; `LOCAL_NAME` children stop with their launcher. Frames report each touched binding's state (running, paused by a gate, invalidated, finished with its pose kept, stopped), refused starts, condition outcomes with the rule's inputs, skipped branch spans, the releaser of each released sequence and the player reference over time, bounded like the trace. An occurrence in the trace ends where it completes or where a stop cuts it short (`AnimationTrace.Stopped` says why, `StoppedBy` the event whose dispatch did it): a STOP_SEQUENCE (0x17) ends its target's current event at the STOP_SEQUENCE's time within the tick; a stop or reset of a binding (by name, by its `LOCAL_NAME` launcher's end, by a cleanup replacing it) ends each of its current events where its sequences freeze; an instance that ends, a cleanup a restart abandons and an event that fails end what still ran. An event that stops its own sequence or binding completes. Only a LOOP that goes round stays open (its sequence starts again). Evidence only: neither the run nor the duration analysis reads it.
- Approximations: `RANDOM_WEIGHT` draws from a table seeded by the scenario's seed (random in the game); range and zone gates are retested every tick (the game staggers them); the player tank is not simulated, so a reset restoring a game-driven node (the tank after callback 17) shows its authored rest pose; cover tests use polygons at the nearest LOD only (nodes with intersection bounds 0x20 are tested by their polygons), take a surface's active state from the run (a surface hidden at load and shown by the run is cover), and report themselves not evaluable in a scene without intersection surfaces; a started range-gated binding is tested from its first tick (the game first tests in its third frame); a manual preview does not run the mission's `ON_STARTUP` and `NEW_GAME_START` entries, so it reports them stopped (flag 0x20) or not started where the game runs them, and a by-name stop of one reads "ignored".
- Playback, seeking and duration analysis advance in 60 Hz ticks; cancellation is observed only between ticks, so a canceled seek leaves the player where an uninterrupted run passes and resuming gives the same frames. A tick refused part-way is reported and never continued; frames are refused until a seek, step or playback replays from a checkpoint or a reset.
- Isolated (compiled) previews are driven by an `AnimationScenario.Isolated` scenario: one start (the animation at 0 s), every call (`AnimationExecutionPolicy.AllCalls`) except the excluded paths (excluding a path excludes what lies below it; including a path below an excluded one includes the calls leading to it; a call cycle, back-links followed, runs until its path would pass the allowance, where that call is excluded as too deep), range gates ignored by default. The scenario's custom player is the player, from which range conditions measure and with which range gates apply as in the game when the rule says so; without one, range conditions measure from the player at start, so an opened binding plays wherever it is. Beams aim at the player.
- Duration analysis (`AnimationPlayer.MeasureDuration`) is the scenario's **Auto playback range** (owner decision 2026-10-29): an independent simulation on a snapshot (current phase, seed, conditions, ground collision, height, the player and beam origin, and finite policy when present) that never mutates source data, advances the visible player or emits sound. It runs until the run's timed part is over (no scheduled start pending, no binding left on authored time) and no pass off it can still reach an event an authored clock starts (a look-ahead, below), and then ends: **5 s after the last timed event** (`OpenEnded`, with `LastTimedSeconds`; a sound or effect tail or a constant spin's first revolution that outlasts those 5 s ends it instead and the explanation says so, `· sound tail to 5.550 s`, `· spins: rotate_piece1 and rotate_piece2 once round to 34.583 s` with `EndsAtSpin`) when something without a timed end still runs or waits (procedural motion without a timer that is no constant spin: debris falling until it lands, a launch, a flight; events after a contact; a range- or zone-gated binding; a sequence still waiting for the release the run never gave it, in a binding that still runs on authored time: a finished, stopping or paused binding can never release it, and one off authored time waits on the events that took it there); else, while a sequence still loops forever or spins or a call cycle still goes round, where every loop has gone round **once** (`Looping`, `Loops: … once round`, `Spins: … once round`); else at the run's end: its last event, with its sound and effect tails (`Finite`). Several starts and calls take the latest; every range that is not a run's own end is at least 5 s. A **timed event** is an occurrence whose dispatch time authored clocks alone set: a scheduled start; an event that waited for its animation- or sequence-clock threshold on a clock that started on authored time (the animation clock when its binding started on authored time and no gate froze it; the sequence clock when, in addition, the pass began on authored time), on any pass; and, on a pass on authored time, an event that a START_TIME threshold (animation, sequence or after-previous clock) or its predecessor let start and the end of an instant event, keyframes, an interpolated transform, a fade, a beam, a light, a camera FOV or screen curve, or procedural motion with a timer (0x400). What an event starts (a call, a reset's cleanup), releases (CALL_SEQUENCE), stops or resets runs on that event's timeline, and a binding's natural end follows its latest completion. A sequence pass leaves authored time, with everything it then starts, when it runs procedural motion without a timer, other than a constant spin, past its dispatch tick (or one a contact or the Y limit ends before its timer), resumes after a waited call whose child ended untimed, was released by a ground contact, or its binding is paused by a gate; it comes back to authored time at an event that waited for such an authored clock (a landing ended only the motion before it: a piece that lands at 3.7 s and changes at 8 s on its animation clock changes at 8 s, a timed event), so while it can still reach one (later in the pass up to its LOOP, its threshold still ahead) the analysis follows it. That look-ahead ignores branches and is not itself timed work: unless a timed point comes while it lasts (the event dispatched), the range ends as it would have where the rest of the timed part was over, so an event the run never dispatches changes nothing (a piece that lands at 0.9 s before an IF the run finds false, around an event at 3 s on its animation clock, still ends 5 s after the last timed event). An after-previous threshold counts from its predecessor's end, so after an untimed end it stays untimed, as does an event whose authored threshold had already passed when its predecessor ended. **One loop iteration**: a sequence loops forever when its LOOP has no count (65535) or no time limit and sends it back to its first event still running (an ON_CALL sequence goes back to waiting instead); its first iteration ends when that LOOP first fires, at least 1 s after its pass began (the player runs at least one tick a pass, so a one-tick loop shows for a second wherever it starts), and its later passes, with what they start or release, repeat it and are not counted; a call into a binding already on its caller's chain (a back-link the scenario follows) closes a call cycle, whose first round ends at that call. A **constant spin** (`AnimationPlayer.SpinRevolution`; owner decision 2026-10-29) loops forever too: procedural motion without a timer that only turns its node, XYZ_ROTATION (0x20) with an Euler rate (byte 136) or acceleration (byte 148) on some axis and nothing else that changes it (gravity alone moves nothing): no TRANSLATION or TRANSLATION_RANGE (4, 8: a fall or a flight, whose landing or limit ends its turning too, stays motion without a timed end), no FORWARD_ROTATION or distance turn (0x80, 0x40, which turn across a direction of travel), no scale or morph rate. Nothing but a stop or a reset ends it (the radar dishes, the copter rotors, m2 `comdish_rotate`, the m4 bridge's `rotate_piece*`, which tumble in their own sequences while `move_piece*` falls). Its first iteration is its first revolution from its dispatch: the time its fastest axis takes to turn 360° (θ = ωt + αt²/2 solved, the player's integration: 35°/s 10.286 s; 3.5°/s gaining 0.4°/s² 34.569 s), at least 1 s; the pass repeats only the turning, so what it played before the spin plays once and its sound and effect tails count (a loop's own tails do not: its next pass plays them again). A spin that would take more than a minute to go round once (`MaximumSpinSeconds`: a tiny or degenerate rate) stays motion without a timed end; a spin with a timer is timed as other timed motion. A sequence whose first event waits for a sequence-clock or after-previous threshold never starts (those clocks run only once its first event has started) and is no work. `DependencyBlocked` (an excluded waited call blocks a sequence), `Unavailable` and `AnalysisLimit` keep their meaning. The safety nets stop timed work that never ends (a call cycle, a loop with a huge count) or that is too heavy to follow within the work limit (a large destruction with every call included can be); the range then ends where the analysis stopped (at least 5 s) as `AnalysisLimit`, whose explanation names the limit, never the 5 s rule (the work and one-hour limits reached during a look-ahead leave the end it began on). They are one hour of simulation and a deterministic work limit (`AnimationPlayer.DurationWorkLimit`) counted in units that follow what each tick does, weighted by their cost (about a nanosecond a unit on a current desktop): the tick, the instances and effects it holds, the events it dispatches (more for a start, more again for a call), the nodes an origin-collision floor query considers, the surface and collision queries with the branches and triangles they test, the collision triangles built at run time, ground-contact mesh vertices and the nodes the settled-ground pass visits. A scenario therefore always stops at the same time, whatever the machine does meanwhile (a cold start, a busy view). A 15 s wall-clock backstop only stops a machine far slower than the limit assumes: its result is kept and marked (`AnimationDuration.TimedOut`, its explanation says it depends on the machine), never measured again by itself; Auto measures again. Both previews measure in the background, never waiting playback for it: a compiled preview as it loads, a source map as soon as Animation mode opens a definition or binding (without preparing, playing or seeking the run, moving the view or evaluating paths; the run's later preparation reuses the context the measurement prepared), and both again when the scenario changes; a newer measurement cancels an older one, a rebuild clears the measurement (and measures the rebuilt map) and a save keeps it. Until the result arrives Auto has no end to show (`of 0–… s`, `rangeState.measuring`; a source map plays to a provisional 30 s meanwhile, a compiled preview to its provisional or previous end). Never assume one fixed duration for every asset.
- Isolated transport: the Animation mode panel's transport, in seconds. Space on a selected animation in Assets or passive preview content plays or pauses without moving focus or stealing native text, button or slider behaviour; during loading it queues playback (a second press cancels; a selection change cancels); modifiers and key repeat never toggle. Steps (one 1/60 s tick) pause and clamp to the range; Stop returns to 0 s of the selected phase; Play at the endpoint restarts; Loop range repeats the range independently of authored loop events. Source maps use Animation mode's transport and the static-baseline Stop above.
- Bind root chooser: indexes each actor root once and lists the first 500 matching scene nodes in scene order with complete labels and node identities.
- Preparation limits refuse new work with a diagnostic before anything is published, never a partial context: up to 256 simultaneous animation instances; 10,000 dispatches per tick; binding/initialization work 96 Mi units per operation (6 Ki per node in scenes over 16,384 nodes) and frame traversal 64 Mi; 256 MiB of retained player state per player (optional checkpoints dropped first, so a seek may replay from earlier); 256 MiB each for decoded textures and for prepared meshes per viewport; 512 MiB of retained audio PCM; a 1,000,000-unit lighting allowance per frame. These are preview limits, not limits on authored game data.
