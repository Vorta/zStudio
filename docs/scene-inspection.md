# 3D inspection and mission coordinates

Whole world, model and animation previews share a fixed top-right inspection panel (a source map docks it as its right Properties pane instead). Parent or Parents names the relationship of the selected object; source-map inspection uses the authored source-tree occurrence. A clicked mesh inside a whole object is identified separately by Surface node and Surface parent(s). glTF node indices and compiled source-node slots are explicitly labelled. Shared compiled nodes can have multiple parents. Its permanent upper readout separates the pointed triangle's world XYZ from the object's world origin. Empty space, leaving the viewport or entering the panel clears the live hit to neutral placeholders without moving or hiding the readout. Hover information stays independent of selected-object details below it. Picking includes transparent triangles and does not test individual texture alpha pixels. Grid, horizon and editor helpers do not supply geometry hits.

In compiled Whole world, enable **Unlock editing**, then left-click a surface or AI marker to pin a floating card and show its selection bounds. Selecting a pickup or vehicle child outlines the complete placed instance. Other geometry outlines the selected subtree; AI nodes receive a marker outline. Locked compiled maps retain hover information and source/tree inspection, but cannot open object cards through clicks, tree selection or MCP. Unlocking alone selects nothing. Turning the toggle off closes the card, bounds and handles after pending drafts are resolved; Cancel keeps the previous unlocked state. Source maps have no lock or **Unlock editing** button: click an object and edit its fields in the card (objects that cannot be edited yet show them read-only, without Revert or Apply). Model and animation cards do not require this map toggle.

Selecting an object expands its details downward below the hover readout. The panel stays at the top right when the camera or object moves, with offscreen/inactive labels when appropriate. Its width and scrollable details height are bounded by the viewport, leaving the navigation cube clear. Short viewports keep a usable details area by placing the cube beside the panel; constrained widths reserve a compact cube slot. The cube returns to its usual corner when space permits or details close. Copy buttons preserve numeric round-trip precision and coordinate-space labels. Animation cards distinguish runtime instance IDs from original source nodes; expired instances do not silently bind to another copy. Inspection does not change animation events or playback.

Floating over the view, the panel is translucent: the scene behind it shows blurred under an 80% tint of the theme background (docked in a source map it is opaque). The probe strip at the top shows, on one line, the name of whatever the cursor points at and the surface point under it, with X, Y and Z coloured like the handles; outside source worlds a second row gives the object's origin. The card header shows the object's icon, name and type (for a source object, its class and node number, with the model in its tooltip), with Isolate (not in a source map, whose World tree isolates) and Close at the top right; its details sit in collapsible sections (a source object's Object, Transform with the Move/Rotate toggles in its header, Zone, Flags as check boxes, and Surface; a placement's Details, with the toggles in its header). The footer shows the draft's status with **Revert** and **Apply**, enabled once something changes; editable placements also take input directly, and showing a placement's handles starts its draft (hiding them again ends it while it changes nothing). **Isolate** (model and Whole world previews) shows only the selected object, a pickup or vehicle as its whole placement; pressed again it shows everything (`scene_selection` `isolate`/`show_all`). Neither moves the camera; Frame all stays a separate command. Source worlds have no Scene or Selection menu: their textures are the project's PNGs, and the World tree's eyes (middle-click isolates) and the **Show all** eye in its header replace Selection. The title trims with a full-name tooltip. Its body has a separate scrollbar gutter, keeping per-field copy buttons clear of the scrollbar. Readouts use subdued, selectable fields, with stacked X/Y/Z components for vectors. The card keeps the same size and field positions when a draft starts or ends.

The mouse wheel scrolls the card when the pointer is over its contents or scrollbar, including coordinate fields. Reaching a scroll limit does not zoom the scene. Camera gestures begin on the scene outside the card; native card buttons, fields and scrollbar retain their own input.

Drag the expanded panel's bottom grip to resize it vertically. Its normal minimum is 216 DIP, half its default total height of 432 DIP; its maximum is 80% of the 3D viewer's height. In shorter viewers the maximum takes precedence over the minimum. The pointer readout and action header remain fixed while the remaining content scrolls. The grip also supports Up/Down (10 DIP) and Home/End (current limits). Resizing does not move the camera or commit draft input. One preferred height is saved for all three 3D viewers; automatic shrinking and closing the details retain it. Reset layout restores 432 DIP.

Closing the card clears its tree selection and Frame selected target and collapses only the details section; the permanent hover readout remains. Compatible preview refreshes restore a retained source row's framing and inspection without selecting an arbitrary runtime copy. Isolating a pickup child keeps the complete placed pickup, consistently through the toolbar and MCP `scene_selection` (a source map's World tree has no isolation for units and pickups). Geometry shared by multiple mission actors has no uniquely editable placement and stays read-only.

Details include model/material/stored texture and LOD, node/parent identity, soil, CanModify and ClipTo. **Zone** is the selected object's built zone ID (Any is 255), shown as **Inherit (ID)** when its source node inherits it, and **Altitude zone check** its independent gate; a clicked internal mesh adds **Surface zone** and **Surface altitude zone check**, and the clicked polygon its ordered **Polygon zones** (a polygon with no zones differs from Any); **Object polygon zones** counts the selected object's polygons per zone set, one line each ("Zone 7: 89 polygons"), with a total for two or more. In a source world, **Zone source** (on read-only objects and in `scene_inspect`) names where the value comes from: the node itself, the reference node whose placement overrides assigned it, the ancestor it inherits from (including groups the build removes) or the load root; an editable object's card shows an inherited zone's source under its **Zone** field instead (for example `Zone 3 · inherited from g101`, with the full source in its tooltip, until the field changes). Compiled worlds show these read-only; a source object's card edits its zone and gate in the **Zone** section (below), and the Zones group's **Edit zones** tool paints polygon zones. AI cards retain ordered directed links, negative sentinels and the raw node integer. Pickups include placement identity, amount and respawn metadata. Tanks include their AIV placement, template, heading and difficulty scope. Scene local, rendered world and authored placement coordinates are labeled separately.

## Document scene hierarchy

**Document scene** shows an expandable hierarchy for the active 3D preview, including placed mission objects. With an animation open, it shows the stable bound scene; spawned copies and runtime reparenting remain separate in the runtime inspection card. When no compatible 3D preview is active, it shows the selected file's stored nodes.

Rows include names, classes, node indices and child counts. Expand arrows and indentation show parent-child relationships. Shared references appear beneath each parent and carry a shared label; world-partition references are labeled separately. **Unlinked nodes** keeps disconnected roots and cyclic components accessible. Invalid references, cycles and the 256-level display limit are explicit leaves. Tooltips distinguish tree parents, stored parents, source nodes, models and mission placements.

Select a row to inspect it and make it the Frame selected target. Selection does not move the camera or open/retarget Properties. Nodes without rendered geometry remain selectable and clear an unrelated geometry card. Static-view Isolate includes the selected parent's descendants. Outside source worlds, use the row context menu or Alt+Enter for independently pinned Properties. In source worlds, selection shows the object card in the docked Properties pane and Alt+Enter focuses it. Clicking geometry or selecting a runtime card reveals its scene node without switching Navigator tabs or choosing a different runtime copy. Tree expansion and selection persist through presentation changes and compatible preview refreshes for the document's lifetime; mission matches use provenance rather than clone indices. Presentation-only expansion, collapse and reveal never commit pending input; collapsing a selected branch retains the hidden descendant's source selection. Intentional node selection uses the existing draft guards.

## Source map editor

Individual source-map pickup rows use the supplied type artwork, with `A` (Ammo) or `W` (Weapon) immediately left of the difficulty label, such as `A E/M/H`. Health (`NANITE`, `NANITE100`, `NANO-CANISTER`) and technology (`PUP_AMPHIB`, `PUP_HOVER`, `PUP_SUB`) have no letter. Icons keep their original colours and proportions in a 20-DIP square within the compact row. Weapon and ammunition variants share family artwork; ERFPG uses the red secondary-mode icon. The type comes from the actual pickup catalog/placement, including entries placed only on other difficulties, rather than the generated scene name. MCP `scene_tree` returns `pickupType` and `pickupKind` (`ammo`, `weapon`, `health`, `technology`) for these rows, null otherwise. The Pickups header keeps its section icon. These are presentation properties, not source edits.

An open source map uses its own editor layout. Below the title (the map's name, with an **Edited** pill while the project has unsaved changes) a command bar holds the scope (back arrow, **Whole world** and, below it, the world's source model file such as `m1.gltf`); **Save**, **Undo** and **Redo**; **LOD** and **Difficulty**; then the view's commands in labelled groups: **Camera** (Frame all, Flycam), **Display** (Wireframe, Textures, Bounds, Horizon), **Overlay** (Non-default soils, CanModify, ClipTo, Landmarks, Face orientation), **Show** (VTOL route) and **Zones** (**Edit zones**, below). The zone and AI network overlays are switched in the World tree's **Zones** and **AI Networks** rows (below). Toggled commands take the accent colour; every command has a tooltip, and most icon tooltips (command bar, outliner and Properties) show as soon as they are pointed at.

The **World** outliner is on the left, the view in the middle and the docked **Properties** pane on the right; the gaps between them resize the panes for the session. The status bar names the selection ("sontur_102 · node #3629" or "No selection"), the mission start and difficulty, and the instance and mesh batch counts; on the right are **Preview notices** (with their count) and the **World tree**, **Properties** and **Diagnostics** pane toggles. Hiding a pane gives its width to the view; hiding Properties hides the selected object's form too (it never floats over the view). The Properties pane's sections fill its width; when they overflow, a scroll bar takes its own column beside them instead of covering them. Diagnostics is the bottom pane: the file and preview problems alone, always open (no collapse), without the compiled explorer's tabs (no Related or Bytes) or its maximize and hide buttons; the status bar's toggle shows and hides it. The status bar shows no messages: a failure in the map (a terrain brush stroke, a conversion whose sources changed after it was planned, Properties that cannot open during a drag or Flycam) is listed in Problems and opens Diagnostics.

The outliner's rows show each node's type by icon and colour: the world, groups (folders: groups and level-of-detail nodes), geometry, empty nodes (a dashed cube: an Object3D with no geometry and nothing below it, which draws nothing but keeps its flags and zone; it shows its zone, has no Frame or eye, and its Properties subtitle reads "Empty Object3D"), placed objects and other nodes (lights, cameras…). Objects and geometry in the world show their zone at the right ("Zone: 12", "Zone: Any"); the units and pickups the mission places after the world is loaded (**AI Vehicles** and **Pickups**, the `aiv` and `puppies` passes) show none, while the objects of **Animations (keyframed)** (the `anim` pass) are world nodes and keep theirs. **AI Vehicles** and **Pickups** list every difficulty's units and pickups whichever difficulty the view shows: each shows at its right the difficulties that place it (`E/M/H`, `M/H`, `H`…; a unit or pickup is the same one in several difficulty resources when its template or type, original position and rotation match, as for placement edits). Those the shown difficulty does not place are dimmed and cannot be selected, framed or hidden; their tooltip names the difficulties to choose. They are ordered by the first difficulty that places them, then by their record there, so switching difficulty keeps the order. A moving-ball icon after a name marks an object a mission animation binds to by name; its tooltip names the animations. The later passes have their own icons: AI vehicles (a tank), pickups (a power-up) and map animations (a film strip). The world, the sections and the passes start collapsed. The info button beside **Show all** opens a legend of these icons. A group's eye hides everything below it; rows inside it show as hidden too. Groups show no counts. **Collapse all** folds every row; **New group** is not available yet. A search turns the tree into a flat list of matching nodes with their parent paths; choosing one selects it. While the world rebuilds after an edit, the tree keeps its rows, disabled, until the rebuilt world is shown. Each row, and each search match, ends, at the pane's right edge, with **Frame selection** (selects the row and frames it) and an eye, both always shown where a row has them; the eyes keep their ordinary colour (the Zones and AI Networks eyes too). The eye hides that object in the view only: no source changes, no undo step; a hidden row is drawn faint. Middle-clicking a world object's eye hides every other object instead (isolation: the eye shows the Isolate icon unless the row is hidden too), and middle-clicking it again shows them again. A group's isolation shows what the tree holds below it; a refresh of the view (difficulty, LOD) ends it, and a rebuild after an edit ends any isolation, while hidden rows stay hidden. A hidden group the build removes hides what it holds. **Show all**, the eye in the World tree header beside **Collapse all**, shows every hidden object (units and pickups too) again and ends isolation; zones and AI networks keep their own eyes. The **AI Vehicles** and **Pickups** rows have a master eye that hides or shows all their units or pickups, which are shown and hidden one by one too (they have no isolation). A master eye (also on **Zones** and **AI Networks**) shows what pressing it does next: it changes when pressed, or when everything below it comes to be shown, or hidden, one by one (a mix leaves it as it was). A section or pass row is never drawn faint. MCP: `scene_tree` rows carry `zone`, and the aiv and puppies rows `difficulties` (with `otherDifficulty: true` and a `placement` summary for those the view does not show, which `select` refuses); `read` with `search` (optionally narrowed by `types`: the legend's group, geometry, empty, object, node, zone, network and definition categories), and `hide`/`show`/`isolate` on a row (rows report `hidden` and `isolated`); `scene_selection` `show_all`.

The tree's rows come in a fixed order: **World**, **Zones**, **AI Vehicles**, **AI Networks**, **Pickups**, **Animations (keyframed)** and **Animations (definitions)**. A section row shows its count; a click on it (or on a definition folder or file row) opens or closes it, and these rows are never the selection. Selecting a zone, network or definition ends the object selection and opens its card in the Properties pane; Escape, **Clear selection** or a click on empty space in the view clears it. Keyboard: Up/Down, Home/End and Page Up/Down move through every shown row, never stopping on a row's buttons; a row that can be selected becomes the selection, while a section, folder, file, pass or note row only takes focus (an outline) and the selection keeps its highlight and card. Left/Right step through the row's buttons (the Zones row's switch, Frame, the eye; Left from the first returns to the row), showing their tooltips; Space or Enter opens or closes a row with children, or presses the button; Escape returns from a button to its row. After a rebuild the tree that had the keyboard gives it to the selected row. MCP: `scene_tree` `sectionRow` describes these rows, `select` (and `properties`, which also focuses the card) opens a card, `hide`/`show` work their eyes (`isolate` refuses zones, networks, units and pickups), `preview_state` `sections` reports the zones and networks shown, the master eyes, emphasis, outlines and selection, and `drafts`/`resolve_drafts` `target: "section"` cover the card's pending input.

- **Zones** lists the zones the map's polygons and objects use, and zones that only have a label, each with its colour square, label and polygon count (as placed: a model placed twice counts twice); polygons without a zone end the list with a dashed square. Each zone's colour is shown or hidden on its own with its eye, and every zone starts hidden; a hidden zone's row is drawn faint. While any zone shows, the zone overlay is on (the overlay the compiled explorer's Object zones and Polygon zones toggles show), in the kind the header's **Objects | Polygons** switch chooses; the switch is disabled while no zone shows. The header's master eye shows or hides every zone. Choosing another overlay (Soils, CanModify, ClipTo, Landmarks) hides every zone. The eyes pause, dimmed, while Edit zones is on, as the tool draws its own zone view. With a shown zone selected, that zone is drawn strong and the others faint; selecting a zone does not show it. Its card edits the **Label** (Enter or Apply saves it as one undo step, empty removes it, Escape or Revert restores it; labels are saved in the map's master model), says what uses the zone (polygons and placements of how many models; objects with their own zone or inherited, empty nodes included) and offers **Paint with this zone**, which engages Edit zones with it. While Edit zones is on, selecting a zone makes it the zone to paint, and a brush marks it in the tree. MCP: `source_zone_label`.
- **AI Networks** lists the mission's AI networks by file (`net_01`…) with their node counts and a square in their attack strategy's colour (the legend lists the strategies). Each network is shown or hidden on its own with its eye, and every network starts hidden; while any shows, the AI overlay is on. The header's master eye shows or hides every network. Nodes and links are always drawn through geometry: at full strength in front of it and at half strength where it hides them. The overlay chip counts the networks shown ("AI networks · all 12", "3 of 12"). Selecting a network shows it (its eye opens), emphasised: larger nodes and bolder links, the others faint; selecting one of its nodes in the view emphasises it too. Its card shows the file and **Net Name**, then one **Behaviour** block with the header settings as stored, named as the developers' own Net Edit dialog (kept in the game's executable) names them: **Net Type**, **Attack Strategy**, **Pursuit Radius Min** and **Max**, **Activation Radius**, **Attack Radius**, **Return Radius**, **Attack Dwell**, **Post Attack Dwell** and **Buddies Attack**, then, under **No effect in play**, **Buddies Activate**, **Path Width** and **Hide Time 1** and **2**, which the retail game never uses. The line under each field says what it does in play and what 0 or a missing value does; the type's, strategy's and pursuit lines follow the type and strategy chosen in the draft, and a pursuit value the strategy ignores is drawn softer. An empty box shows the game's value as a hint, and a label's tooltip names the stored key. Buddies Attack counts only while Buddies Activate is stored: turning it on stores both, and a file that has it on without Buddies Activate shows a bar whose **Store it** checks it. A field stored twice (or not as plain values) is read-only, with a note. Then come the nodes, links and the network's diagnostics, and other stored keys read-only. Input is checked as it is typed (a name of at most 19 letters, digits, `_`, `-` or `.`; a type and strategy from their lists; numbers of 0 or more, a path width above 0); Apply or Enter accepts all changed fields as one undo step ("Edit net_05: Attack Strategy"), changing only those values in `data/mN/zrdr/aipath/net_NN.zrd`: a stored value is replaced in place and a missing key is added before the nodes, keeping comments and layout. The file name is the network's ID, which vehicles name, so it is not edited; a network stored as binary data is read-only. Nodes are still moved by selecting them in the view. MCP: `source_ai_network`.
- **Animations (definitions)** lists the `.zad` files the mission's animation list (`anim.zad`) names, grouped by folder (the mission's own first) and file, with each definition by its animation name, and listed files that are missing. A file listed twice shows twice, as it is compiled twice. A definition whose root name no node of this world has (so the map has no animation from it) is dimmed. Its card is read-only: the file, its place in it and what lists it; the binding (root name, attach node, the objects it binds to, each a link that selects it); its settings (activation, health, reset time, execution, priority, prerequisites); its sequences with their event counts; and its keyframe scripts. The objects it binds to are outlined in gold in the view (the first 64).

Over the view: a **Perspective** chip (top left) opens the projection, named views and framing; the navigation cube sits top right; the selected object and its shown gizmo are at the bottom left; the surface under the cursor (its name and X/Y/Z) at the bottom right; and chips name the zone overlay and, while AI nodes are on, the networks shown. **Flycam** frames the view in the accent colour and shows a banner ("Press Esc to return to normal controls", with the speed and **Exit**); Escape ends it (the banner's **Exit** works through UI automation; Flycam holds the mouse).

**Properties** shows the selection's type icon, name and "Object3D · node #N", with **Clear selection (Esc)** (Frame selection and isolation are in the World tree's rows). With nothing selected the pane stays, showing a hint, **Recently selected** (the last four objects; one click selects one again, found by its tree identity, never its name) and a summary of the world. Escape clears the selection when no field, drag or Flycam uses it, and so does a click on empty space in the view (a press and release without dragging that no object, handle or tool takes; MCP: `scene_card` `clear`).

## Source-world object editing

Source-world selection opens the object form in the Properties pane, titled with the object's name, with its node type and built node as small read-only text below (the tooltip names its model or source file). Its fields are editable at once: Name and referenced Model (with a browse button for a project model) where they can change, the stored local **Position**, **Rotation (degrees)**, **Scale**, supported flags (check boxes) and class-specific commands. Tree nodes without visible geometry, including World, Light and Camera, use the same panel. Source-only groups and removed reference holders can only be renamed (right-click in the tree); their other edits are unavailable. Alt+Enter focuses this panel; the source-world tree no longer has a Properties menu. Other viewers keep their independently pinned Properties window. Generated terrain retains **Edit terrain…** and its dedicated editor.

The first change opens one draft of all pending fields; the footer then reads **Unsaved changes** and enables **Revert** and **Apply** (both stay disabled while nothing differs). The footer's buttons are icons whose tooltips name them as soon as they are pointed at: **Copy…** and **Delete** (an object's), then **Revert** and **Apply**. Enter and focus changes do not accept the draft; Escape in a text field restores that field (the Object zone box keeps its input). The Transform header's **Move** and **Rotate** toggles show those handles, and the same toggle hides them; both can be off. Dragging an X/Y/Z letter sideways changes that number (0.1 per pixel, rotation 0.5°, scale 0.01; Shift ×10, Ctrl ×0.1; Escape during the drag restores it). Move arrows point along the object's own axes (they turn with it); rotation rings turn it about its origin around world X, Y and Z. Handle drags write the local Position and Rotation (degrees) into the fields, and typed values move the handles. Apply validates and accepts one source transaction, undo step and world update; input it cannot accept shows its reason on the card and the draft stays (MCP: `invalid_draft`). Revert restores the accepted fields. MCP: `scene_card` `set` with `fields` and/or `transformMode` (`move`, `rotate` or `none`), then `apply` or `cancel` (Revert); `begin` only checks that an edit could start. CanModify and ClipTo are exclusive source modes. Copy and Delete resolve pending input first. **Copy…** asks for the copy's name and checks it as it is typed (1–32 printable characters without spaces, commas, quotes or `#` `%` `;`, not ending like an editor's `.001` copy suffix, and no name a node of the world has), showing the reason below the name and keeping **Copy** disabled until it is valid; a copy the edit refuses shows its reason there too, and the window closes once the copy is accepted and the world starts rebuilding (a failure after that shows on the card). The copy stands beside the original: its local X plus the object's width along its parent's X (the extent of its shown meshes), with Y, Z, rotation and scale kept, and it is selected once the world shows it. An object without a visible width (a Light, a Camera or an empty group) is copied in place. MCP: `source_world_object_edit` `duplicate` with `beside: true`, which refuses such an object (`unsupported`) instead; give it a position.

The object form replaces duplicate readouts: Position is the stored local value, and rendered **Object world origin XYZ** and **Compiled source node** are omitted. Clicked surface coordinates, normals and textures are under the collapsed **Surface** section. The **Zone** section separates the **Object zone** (the editable choice; the renderer draws an object only while its zone is current) from the **Polygon zones** (the zones a polygon makes current when the camera or a vehicle is on it) of a placed object, group or geometry, under a divider and its own sub-header (an info tooltip, and the total when there are two or more rows): one row per zone set of all the object's polygons, its own and those below it, most first. A row shows a swatch in the overlay's colours (striped in stored order for several zones), the zones (**Zone 7**, **Zones 7, 9**, **Any** with "No zone limit", **No zones** with a dashed swatch), their labels and the count ("1 polygon", "89 polygons"); after 16 rows come **N more zone sets** and **Unknown zones**, and a count that stopped early ends in "+" with a note. An object without polygons says "No polygons here or below." The list is plain text; right-click **Copy polygon zones** copies it. An inherited object zone's hint reads, for example, "Zone 3 · inherited from g101", with the full source in its tooltip; a read-only object zone (terrain, script-created nodes) is plain text too.

The **Zone** section edits the selected object's zone. **Object zone** is an editable combo box: **Inherit** (take the parent's zone), **Any** (not limited to a zone), or a typed zone number from 0 to 254; below it, "Zone 0–254, Any or Inherit" turns into "Enter 0–254, Any or Inherit" for anything else, and Apply refuses it; the section header repeats the value. A load root has no parent, so it offers only Any or a number. **Altitude zone check** (ground only while its zone is current) is the zone's gate: vehicles and the game camera find the object's ground height only while their current zones allow its zone (include it or Any, or there are none yet); off, it is ground in every zone, even where it is not drawn. Drawing, collision, line of sight, camera obstruction, proximity and turrets follow the zone either way. It matters where surfaces overlap in plan view (stacked floors, tunnels, interiors, bridges), where the ground found also sets the current zones; Any behaves the same either way, and a parent with the check on whose zone is not current keeps everything below it from being found. Its tooltip says so. Both join the card's draft and apply with its other fields as one undo step, written where that occurrence keeps its zone: on the node itself, in the reference placement's override, or on the load root. A zone several placements, copies or missions share asks before changing all of them (MCP: `scene_card` `apply` with `sharedScope: true`; without it the edit is refused as `shared_scope`). Terrain zones stay in their recipe and script-created nodes have none; their cards show the zone read-only. Polygon zones are painted with the polygon zone tool (below).

The **Zones** group's **Edit zones** toggle (or **Tools → Paint polygon zones** / **Erase polygon zones**, P and E) engages the polygon zone tool in the Whole world of a source map. Engaged, the group shows a **Paint | Erase** switch and the zone picker; the view takes an accent frame and a banner (mode, zone and its label, keys, **Exit**), the tool draws its own zone view, the chosen zone strong and the others faint, while the overlays (Overlay and the World tree's zone colours) pause, their buttons disabled until it is left, and models the tool cannot paint are drawn faded, the pointer passing through them. A left-button drag paints the zone on (or erases it from) every polygon the pointer crosses; touched polygons show the result at once (a polygon left without zones darkens), and release applies the stroke as one undo step ("Paint zone 1 on 14 polygons"); strokes queue while earlier ones apply, so painting goes on, and Undo/Redo wait until they have. Hovering outlines the polygon a click would change (white), leave as it is (grey) or skip because it has three zones (yellow), and the other placements that change with it; the surface readout adds its file, placements and zones. After a stroke, notices report full polygons left as they are (**Show** outlines them) and editable terrain it crossed (terrain keeps its zones in its recipe). Ctrl held inverts the mode for a stroke, Shift+click picks a zone of the polygon under the pointer, [ and ] step through the zones the map uses, and Esc drops a stroke, then leaves the tool (the overlays resume as they were chosen). The zone picker takes 0–254, Any or a label and lists the zones the map's polygons carry as placed in the world with their labels and polygon counts; zones are named in the World tree (a zone's card). Paint adds the zone after a polygon's others, painting Any replaces them and a numbered zone replaces Any; erase removes it. A placement takes its own zones; only the map's own models (`data/mN/models`) take them: shared models in `data/common`, mission vehicles in subfolders and placed units and pickups do not. Engaging it ends Flycam and the terrain brush, and is refused while AI path or VTOL route editing, a drag or unfinished object-card or Properties input is pending; Flycam ends the tool at once; the terrain brush, AI path and VTOL route editing, drop-window moves and starting to edit the selected object's card end it too, and are refused while a stroke is held or strokes are still applying (also after leaving the tool). Released strokes still apply after leaving, in order once other source edits have finished; one released during another operation (such as an export, a check or terrain planning) is refused, as is one an object it painted no longer matches (its map closed, or another edit removed, replaced or renumbered the object; zone edits and labels keep it); neither is retargeted. A stroke takes at most 4,096 polygons on 1,024 placements; the rest is left out with a notice. MCP: `source_polygon_zones`, `source_zone_tool`, `source_zone_label`.

## Compiled-world placement transforms

**Unlock editing** starts off for each new compiled document. Once it is on, a selected placement's authored fields take input at once, and the **Move** / **Rotate** toggles in its details header show or hide the handles; the first input or shown handle starts the draft. While a placement cannot be edited the toggles keep their space, invisible, so fields do not shift. Source-map placements have no lock and take input the same way. Pickups support XYZ position and Euler XYZ rotation; supported AIV vehicles (including tanks) support position and Y heading. AI navigation nodes support position only; Rotate stays hidden for placements without rotation. Other geometry and runtime animation poses remain read-only. Tank templates must resolve unambiguously. Actors without an AIV placement remain inspectable without inventing a placement record.

AIV placement records contain one heading scalar, not XYZ angles. The inspected reconstruction of retail `Player::CreateFromNamesAtPose` (0x421AB0) applies `(0, heading × π/180, 0)` after reading that scalar. Pitch and roll cannot be authored for an individual vehicle through this placement format. Pickup records already contain all three rotation components; these are exposed without changing shared model templates or inventing source fields.

**Authored placement XYZ**, **Authored rotation XYZ (degrees)** or **Heading degrees** become editable in place. **Object world origin XYZ** stays read-only: a selected child mesh can have a different origin from the complete unit. Rotation fields display degrees; pickups retain radians in their source records and vehicle headings retain degrees. Position-only edits preserve untouched angle bytes. Move arrows and rotation rings use world axes around the authored placement origin, without snapping or scaling.

Valid field changes and handle drags update one shared preview draft; the footer enables **Revert** and **Apply** once it differs. Releasing a handle retains the draft; **Apply**, or Enter in a field, accepts position and rotation together as one undo step. Escape during a drag restores that drag's starting transform and retains earlier draft changes. **Revert**, or Escape in a field, discards the whole draft; hiding the handles ends a draft that changes nothing. Incomplete/non-finite inputs retain the last valid preview and hide handles until valid again. Refreshes preserve pending text, selection and scroll position. Per-field copy buttons copy accepted values with coordinate-space labels (`scene_card` copy without a field copies all); native text selection can copy draft input. Navigation, selection, scene refresh, save and close explicitly resolve pending input; focus loss does not confirm it. Existing Properties remains independently pinned.

Pickup counterparts retain the existing original-type/position/rotation matching. Tanks require a unique original template and starting XYZ/heading in each effective difficulty resource. Links survive movement; the same displacement applies to linked records, shared fallback resources move once, and ambiguous/missing counterparts stay unchanged. The card shows affected difficulties before confirmation. AI edits affect one exact source record.

The map document owns these edits. Its Undo/Redo/Save commands handle them chronologically. Pickup, AI and tank transform patches share archive baselines and save together. Only changed position/rotation scalar nodes change; unrelated bytes, AI links, untouched angles, unknown values and archive ordering are preserved. Saves check external changes, stage/reparse/read back archives, and verify unrelated-byte preservation before atomic replacement. Protected datasets require Save As; the new destination becomes the subsequent save target, shown as **Save archive** in the card. Partial multi-file publication reports which files remain dirty and retains every requested destination. Ordinary Save retries unpublished copies as new files without returning to source paths or overwriting competing files. Working snapshots feed dependent previews without changing cached mission baselines.

## MCP

`zstudio_scene_inspect` requires the current `preview` UUID and accepts either an opaque `target` or `screenPoint: [x,y]` in viewport DIP. By default it reads the selected card, falling back to hover; hover state is also returned separately. Results include document/revision, target and draft state. Queries never move/capture the physical pointer. Targets expire with their render instance or AI snapshot.

AI network marker cards include a read-only **Attack strategy** field among the scrolling details below **This AI network node**, immediately above **Status**, including during position editing. It shows the stored network string, **Not stored**, or **Unavailable (invalid data)**; long values use the same bounded wrapping as other scalar fields, with the bounded text available through the tooltip and copy button. All nodes/arrows in a network share the strategy color described in [AI networks](ai-networks.md); the selected marker remains white. `scene_inspect.attackStrategy` and `hoverAttackStrategy` include bounded stored text, status, normalized strategy key, character count/truncation and the network's RGB hex color. Field copy and scene_card copy-all results include the strategy once. The field is absent for non-AI selections, and clearing selection closes the details.

`zstudio_workspace_view` accepts `changes.inspectionPanelHeight` in DIP for the shared preferred total expanded height (default 432, normalized to at least 216). `scene_inspect.panel` reports `preferredHeight`, `effectiveHeight`, `minimumHeight`, `maximumHeight` and `expanded`. Effective limits depend on the current viewport; they do not overwrite the stored preference. These presentation operations require no document revision or draft resolution. They preserve partial fields, draft tokens, selection and history.

An explicit height request or Reset layout supersedes an ongoing resize gesture. Ending or canceling that older gesture cannot overwrite the newer preference. Ordinary viewport resizing only clamps presentation and retains the preferred height.

`zstudio_scene_card` provides these actions:

- `select`: supply `target`, or scene `node` with optional string `runtime` ID. Compiled Whole world rejects selection with `locked` until editing is unlocked; source maps have no lock. `clear` closes the selection.
- `copy`: optional `field` label; omitted copies all details. Returns GUI copy text; `clipboard: true` also writes the clipboard.
- `begin`: requires owning `document` and expected `revision`; starts a placement's draft, as its first field input or shown handle does. A source object's fields are already editable, so for it `begin` only checks that an edit could start.
- `set`: requires document/revision and current `token`. Supply any combination of three `position` strings, pickup `rotationDegrees` (three strings), vehicle `headingDegrees` (one string), and `transformMode: "move" | "rotate" | "none"` (`none` hides the handles). Each input string is bounded to 64 characters and may contain temporary incomplete numeric input. Unsupported rotation axes are rejected before changing the draft. Draft results report rotation axes, mode, bounds and handle visibility.
- `apply` / `cancel`: require document/revision and current draft token. Apply additionally checks the revision captured at draft creation.

`drafts` and `resolve_drafts` accept `target: "scene"`; resolution requires the exact draft token. Existing `undo_redo` and `save_document` handle accepted edits. Stale targets/revisions/drafts, locked coordinate edits and conflicting archive owners are rejected. Discovery remains windowless; local MCP stays opt-in.

The `zstudio_scene_lock` command gates compiled Whole world cards, bounds and transform editing; source worlds have no lock and refuse it as `unsupported`. Pass `locked: false` to enable the GUI's **Unlock editing** toggle, or `true` to turn it off. The `EditingLocked` document-state field reports this shared lock. Lock changes require the current document revision and explicitly resolved drafts. Read-only `scene_inspect` target/point queries and source/tree inspection remain usable while locked. Card scrolling is presentation-only; MCP inspection/copy already exposes its complete contents.

Tokens identify both the draft lifetime and its input: canceling and reopening identical coordinates produces a new token. Old requests cannot apply, change or cancel the new draft. Scene selection and isolation reject active Fly or pickup-drag interactions before changing selection or the camera.

`zstudio_scene_tree` shares Document scene. Supply `document` and default/explicit `action: "read"` for paged roots. The result identifies the hierarchy `context`, optional active `preview`, source, selection and `children` page. Read a row's direct children with its opaque `row` and current context. Rows expose parent occurrence, relation, node/source-node/model identity, shared/reference warnings, child count and expansion/selection state. Use the usual `offset`, `limit` (at most 200) and `query` parameters; queries filter the requested level before paging.

Actions `expand` and `collapse` require a row; `reveal`, `select` and `properties` accept a row or a node index. All actions require the current context and, for preview hierarchies, the returned preview ID. Reveal only expands ancestors and scrolls the row into view; select additionally refuses with `pending_drafts` while a scene-card, AI path or drop-window (Parachute move) draft, or unfinished animation-editor input, is pending, as a GUI selection resolves those; Properties input stays, since choosing an object never retargets Properties (with `properties`, every draft is resolved first). Properties opens the same independently pinned window as the row context menu, including stored nodes without a current preview, and rejects unresolved drafts; in a source map it selects the row and focuses the docked object card instead. Stale contexts and row IDs are rejected. The existing flat `scene_nodes` query remains available.

### Animation association marks

The World outliner and search results use the same animation glyph for initial roots and known indirect recipients. Initial roots retain cyan `#6FC8D6`; indirect-only recipients use `#89B5BC` (half HSL saturation, unchanged hue/lightness/opacity). Any initial-root association wins when an object has both. Tooltips explain the origins and list up to eight associations, with an ellipsis for omitted details. The legend describes both colors.

Discovery uses the selected mission resources and difficulty: turret destruction/firing, vehicle startup/destruction/transitions, pickup collection, concrete weapon flyout model templates, and animation calls or writes to known recipients. Position references and unknown future impact victims do not imply animation ownership. Missing entries and bounded/incomplete discovery are diagnosed; the marker does not mean an animation is playing. This read-only index does not change initial-root definition highlighting, gameplay resources, or export data. MCP `scene_tree` exposes the same origins and reasons.
