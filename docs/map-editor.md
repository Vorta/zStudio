# Map editor

Open a [source project](source-project.md), then a map. You edit the map's sources directly: objects, placements, AI networks, animations and their VTOL drop windows, zones and terrain. Every change is one undo step of the project; **Save** writes the changed source files, and only [export](source-project.md#check-and-export) builds game files. Camera controls are in [3D navigation](3d-navigation.md).

## Open a map

The map picker lists the single-player maps (m1–m6) and the multiplayer maps; one map is open at a time. A map whose model or script is broken still has its button; opening it reports the problem. The view shows the mission's starting scene at the chosen **Difficulty** (Easy, Medium or Hard): the units and pickups that difficulty places and the poses its startup animations set. The back arrow left of **Whole world**, or **File → Close file** (**Ctrl+W**), returns to the picker, asking to save or discard unsaved changes.

MCP: `zstudio_source_world_list`, `zstudio_source_world_open`.

## Layout

- **Title:** the map's name, with an **Edited** pill while the project has unsaved changes.
- **Command bar:** the scope (back arrow, **Whole world** and the world's source model file, such as `m1.gltf`); **Save**, **Undo**, **Redo**; **LOD** (LOD 0 is each object's highest detail) and **Difficulty**; then **Camera** (Frame all, Flycam), **Display** (Wireframe, Textures, Bounds, Horizon), **Overlay** (Non-default soils, CanModify, ClipTo, Landmarks, Face orientation) and **Zones** (Edit zones).
- **World tree** (left), the view (middle) and the **Properties** pane (right). Drag the gaps between them to resize.
- **Over the view:** the **Perspective** chip (projection, named views, framing), the navigation cube, the selected object with its handles, and the surface under the pointer with its X/Y/Z.
- **Status bar:** the selection, the mission start and difficulty, the [player tank](#player-tank) while it isn't drawn as at the start, instance and mesh batch counts, **Preview notices**, and toggles for the **World tree**, **Properties** and **Diagnostics** panes. Diagnostics lists the map's file and preview problems.

### World tree

Rows come in a fixed order: **World** (the authored glTF hierarchy, including groups the build removes), **Zones**, **AI Vehicles**, **AI Networks**, **Pickups**, **Animations** (the `.zad` definitions the mission lists, then Common and the objects placed by animations). Icons show each node's type; the info button beside **Show all** opens a legend.
- Objects show their zone on the right (`Zone: 12`, `Zone: Any`).
- Expand an imported object to browse its groups, nested references, geometry and LOD branches. Clicking a child selects that exact part; clicking geometry in the map still selects the whole object. Hidden parts remain in the tree and can be inspected. Deep branches scroll horizontally to keep names and eye buttons accessible.
- Healthy and destroyed branches keep their initialized visibility: healthy normally has an open eye, destroyed a closed eye. Hide healthy and show destroyed with their separate eyes to inspect the destroyed geometry. These are preview overrides, independent of the stored Visible flag: no source edit or undo step is created. Changing or resetting these eyes keeps the tree at its current scroll position and retains keyboard focus. A child blocked by its parent, isolation or LOD is dimmed; its tooltip explains why. Right-click the object and choose **Reset part visibility** to restore its initialized defaults. Ending isolation preserves the chosen part states. Overrides last for the open map and compatible preview refreshes; changing them stops animation playback, whose own visibility applies when played again.
- **AI Vehicles** and **Pickups** list every difficulty's entries, tagged with the difficulties that place them (`All`, `E/M`, `H`…). Those the shown difficulty doesn't place are dimmed and can't be selected.
- **Pickups** holds three groups: **Weapons**, **Ammo** and **Other** (health and technology), each with its count, Frame and eye. **Group by type** (the toggle on the Pickups row, or its context menu) adds a row per pickup type inside them. A weapon pickup shows the weapon's render from the game manual, ammo its weapon's HUD icon. Pickups are named by type and numbered per type, such as `ERFPG #1` or `Proximity Mine #2`; the game's name (`pu01700`) is in the tooltip.
- An animation icon after a name marks an object a mission animation binds to; at half saturation, an object that receives an animation indirectly (a turret's destruction, a vehicle's startup, a pickup's collection, another animation's call). The tooltip names the animations and why; a click lists them, each with **Open** (Animation mode).
- Each row ends with **Frame selection** and an eye (a definition row has **Open** ▷ and Frame instead). The eye hides the object in the view only (no source change); middle-clicking a world object's eye shows only that object, and again shows everything. **Show all** in the header clears object hiding and isolation; child visibility gates keep their current settings. Use **Reset part visibility** on the object to restore its initialized healthy/destroyed state. The eyes on **Zones**, **AI Vehicles**, **AI Networks** and **Pickups** show or hide everything below them.
- **Collapse all** folds every row. Type in the search box to list matching nodes with their parent paths; every word must match (name, type, pickup kind or game name).
- **Keyboard:** Up/Down, Home/End and Page Up/Down move through the rows; Left/Right reach a row's buttons; **Space** or **Enter** opens a row or presses the button; **Enter** on a definition or binding row opens it in [Animation mode](#animation-mode), **F2** focuses its card. Left/Right also reach the Animations row's **Group by start** toggle.

MCP: `zstudio_scene_tree`, `zstudio_scene_selection`.

Part visibility is also available through `zstudio_source_map_parts` and `zstudio_source_map_parts_visibility`. Tree-selected parts use the usual Properties controls for supported source edits. Shared-model edits affect every use of the owning source; unsupported part operations are disabled rather than applied to the enclosing object.

## Select and inspect

Click an object in the view or the World tree. The Properties pane shows its card: its icon, name and type (with its source file in the tooltip), and collapsible sections. Clicking a mesh inside a turret or vehicle selects the whole object; the clicked polygon is under **Surface**. Clear the selection with **Escape**, **Clear selection** or a click on empty space. With nothing selected, the pane shows **Recently selected**, the **Player tank** and a summary of the world. **Alt+Enter** focuses the card; it opens the Properties window instead for a terrain piece (its recipe), for a pickup, vehicle, object the startup animations place, AI node or AI path selected in the view, and with nothing selected. When a placement can't be edited, its card's **Placement editing** row says why; a unit selected while hidden becomes editable as soon as it shows again (its eye, the end of an isolation or **Show all**).

MCP: `zstudio_scene_inspect`, `zstudio_scene_card` (`select`, `clear`, `copy`).

## Player tank

The player tank's card (the first vehicle the mission spawns, `bft_00` in the shipped maps) also chooses how the view draws it, marked **Preview**: nothing is saved or undone, and Apply and Revert leave it alone. Its **Master type** and **Weapons** come first; **Details** (the placement, and why it can't be edited where it can't) follows, collapsed until you open it.
- **Master type:** **Tracked**, **Amphibious**, **Hover** or **Sub**. Each shows the end of the mission's own transition from tracked, with the tank parked (no wake, floating or diving). A type the map's tank has no transition for is disabled (shipped: m1 tracked only; m2 adds amphibious; m3 and m4 hover; m5, m6 and the multiplayer maps sub). The note under the radios also gives the game's single-player rules, which allow amphibious from mission 3, hover from 4 and sub in 6 only.
- **Weapons:** **Primary** (RFPG or ERFPG) and **Secondary** (the 16 secondary weapons, by weapon slot). The box under them shows the weapon's render, its manual name and the gun model it draws; several weapons share a model (the remote mines the proximity mines', the missiles and nukes one rail). In sub the beam and fire weapons are disabled, and choosing sub with one selected switches to the weapon the game picks.
- The choice lasts while the map is open (rebuilds, undo, save and difficulty keep it); opening a map starts as a new game of that mission does (Tracked, RFPG and HE Mortar in the shipped maps). While the choice differs from that start, the status bar names it, and with nothing selected the pane's **Player tank** row shows it and selects the tank. A change redraws the view; with a pending edit on the card the map first asks to apply or discard it, and Cancel leaves the tank as it was. A mission whose opening drops the tank in (m2, m4, m5) hides it at the start, as the game does.
- The keyboard: **Tab** enters each group at its chosen radio; the arrow keys, **Home** and **End** move and choose.

MCP: `zstudio_scene_options` (`bftType`, `bftPrimary`, `bftSecondary`), `zstudio_preview_state` (`bft`).

## Edit objects

A selected object's fields are editable at once: **Name** and **Model** where they can change, **Position**, **Rotation (degrees)**, **Scale**, its flags and its **Zone**. The first change starts a draft (**Unsaved changes**); **Apply** accepts every changed field as one undo step, **Revert** discards them. **Escape** in a field restores that field.
- **Handles:** the Transform header's **Move** and **Rotate** toggles show the handles. Move arrows follow the object's own axes; rotation rings turn it about its origin. Drag an X/Y/Z label sideways to change that number (0.1 per pixel, 0.5° for rotation, 0.01 for scale; **Shift** ×10, **Ctrl** ×0.1).
- **Flags:** standable (altitude surface), collision (intersection surface), collide by bounding box, proximity, landmark, overwrite, CanModify (craters and quicksand allowed) and ClipTo (blocked). CanModify and ClipTo exclude each other.
- **Zone:** **Object zone** is **Inherit** (the parent's zone), **Any** or a zone from 0 to 254; the renderer draws the object only while its zone is current. **Altitude zone check** makes the object ground only while its zone is current, which matters where surfaces overlap (floors, tunnels, bridges). A zone several placements or missions share asks before changing all of them. **Polygon zones** lists the zone sets of the object's polygons; paint them with the [polygon zone tool](#zones).
- **Copy…** asks for the copy's name and places the copy beside the original (in place for a light, camera or empty group). **Delete** removes the object. Groups the build removes can only be renamed: right-click the row and choose **Rename…**.
- A world, light or camera lists the script commands that set it (such as `WorldSetFogColor 0.5 0.5 0.5`); edit them there.

Where an edit lands:

| Object | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| A node of the mission database (`mN.gltf`) or its parts | the glTF node; a part's node changes in every copy of the part |
| A node of a model file | that file, for every load of it (the card says so) |
| An object a script placed | the script instruction that set the value (such as `Object3DTranslate 110.0 5.0 -50.0`), or a new one after the instruction that made it |

An edit is refused when it would change several objects at once (an instruction that runs more than once, or one taking its value from a macro), when a script or animation finds the object by a name the edit changes, or when the rebuilt world would make any script instruction act on another node. The refusal says what to change first.

MCP: `zstudio_scene_card` (`set`, `apply`, `cancel`), `zstudio_source_world_object`, `zstudio_source_world_object_edit` (`rename`, `model`, `duplicate`, `delete`; `parent` moves an object to another parent, MCP only), `zstudio_source_world_command`.

## Placements

Click a pickup, an AI vehicle or an AI network node, change its fields or drag its handles, then **Apply**. Pickups have a position and Euler XYZ rotation in degrees; AI vehicles a position and Y heading; AI nodes a position. The edit changes the owning resource: `puppies*.zrd` for pickups, `aiv*.zrd` for vehicles, `aipath\net_NN.zrd` for AI nodes.
- **Difficulties:** the same pickup on other difficulties (same type, original position and rotation) moves with it; a vehicle's counterparts need the same template, position and heading. The card names the difficulties it changes.
- **Precision:** edited positions and AIV headings are saved with one decimal, pickup rotations (radians) with four, as the shipped files write them. Drags snap to that precision, so the view shows what is saved. Values an edit doesn't change keep their text, and an integer coordinate that moves becomes a float (`12.0`).

**Add a pickup:** **Tools → Add pickup…** or the Pickups row's context menu. Choose a type, position, rotation, amount (default: the type's amount; 0 uses the game's default), respawn delay and **All**, **Easy**, **Medium** or **Hard**. The map must already load that type's template. Positions are stored as entered (rounded to one decimal), without terrain snapping. Adding to one difficulty creates its own resource file when it shared another's; once saved, that addition can't be undone.

MCP: `zstudio_pickup_move`, `zstudio_scene_card`, `zstudio_source_pickup_add`, `zstudio_pickups`.

## AI networks

The **AI Networks** section lists the mission's networks (`net_01`…) with their node counts and attack-strategy colour. Show each with its eye (all start hidden). Nodes and links are drawn through geometry, at half strength where geometry hides them; arrows show link directions.

| Stored strategy | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| `HEA` (head-on) | orange |
| `CIR` (circle) | cyan |
| `BAC` (back and forth) | purple |
| `FOL` (follow network) | green |
| `ZIG` (zigzag) | pink |
| `SIT` (sit) | yellow |
| not stored | red (the game attacks head-on) |
| empty or unrecognized | grey |

Select a network to emphasise it and open its card. The card edits the network header with the names of the developers' own Net Edit dialog: **Net Name**, **Net Type**, **Attack Strategy**, **Pursuit Radius Min** and **Max**, **Activation Radius**, **Attack Radius**, **Return Radius**, **Attack Dwell**, **Post Attack Dwell** and **Buddies Attack**; under **No effect in play**, **Buddies Activate**, **Path Width** and **Hide Time 1** and **2**, which the game never uses. The line under each field says what it does in play. **Apply** changes only those values in `data\mN\zrdr\aipath\net_NN.zrd`.

Moving, rotating or scaling several nodes at once uses AI path Edit mode, which only MCP turns on (`zstudio_ai_path`); then click, Shift-click or drag a rectangle to select nodes and press **G**, **R** or **S** with the pointer (**X**, **Y**, **Z** constrain to an axis), or type values; **Enter** confirms one undo step, **Escape** cancels.

MCP: `zstudio_source_ai_network`, `zstudio_ai_networks`, `zstudio_ai_nodes`, `zstudio_ai_selection`, `zstudio_ai_path`.

## Animations

The World tree's **Animations** section lists the map's definition files, then **Common** (definitions shared by every map), then the objects placed by animations. A file with one definition shows that definition. Each definition row says how it starts with its icon: **Mission start** (the opening or a saved-game load), **On startup**, **On damage**, **Called** (by another animation of the map), **Started by the game** (pickups, vehicles, turrets, the tank's trails, weapon flyouts) or **No caller found** (nothing in the map calls it; the game may still play it by name). The grey text after the name says what it acts on: the bound object (`rlbridge`), a run of them (`rdrail01–08`, with `×8`), the player tank, the units the game starts it on (`5 vehicles`), its template, or **not in this map**. ⧉ marks a definition whose name an earlier one has (calls, stops and prerequisites reach the first). A definition with several bindings lists them by the name calls use (`rdrail_destroy03 · rdrail03`). **Group by start** (the toggle on the Animations row, or its context menu) groups them by how they start instead of by file. **Frame** shows only for a definition bound to an object the World tree lists. In a pane narrower than 260 DIP the grey text and Frame hide; from 400 DIP the grey text adds how it starts (`rlbridge · ← rdrail_destroy0* (8)`). **Click** a definition to select it: its card opens in Properties and the view outlines the objects it binds to; nothing plays, evaluates or moves the camera. The card is a summary: **Open in Animation mode**, **Bindings** (each bound object, a click selects it, with **Frame**), how it starts (**Activation**), **Calls and callers** and **Source** (its file, line and the maps it affects). It edits no field: its fields are edited in Animation mode's Inspector, where their effect shows; paths and drop windows are Animation mode's too. The row's context menu offers **Open in Animation mode**, **Frame bound objects**, **Select** its object (when it binds one the World tree lists), **Copy animation name**, **Show source** (the definition's source text in a flyout) and **Properties** (**Alt+Enter**: its card); a binding's offers Open, Frame and Select its object, and Copy animation name. The World tree's search finds a definition by its name, its NAME, what it acts on, its file, its bindings' names or how it starts ("damage", "startup"); its results list the objects, then the animations with **Open**, what matched underlined and, when it is not the name, which field it was.

With an object selected, the animations bound to it carry an amber bar (a closed file holding one, an amber dot). In Animation mode, with the World tree shown, the opened definition's **Open** is pressed; after **Exit**, a dot marks the definition whose scenario is kept.

The card's **Open in Animation mode**, the row's **Open** (▷), **Enter** or a double-click, the row's context menu, **Animation → Open in Animation mode**, and the **Open** of an object card's **Animations affecting this object** (every object's card ends with it, the player tank's too: the first 8, then **Show n more**) or of the list an object row's animation icon opens (every animation, scrolling, never cut short) all enter Animation mode. A binding row's **Open** enters it on that binding.

### Animation mode

Animation mode is a layout of the map editor for one opened definition. The 3D view stays on top with its camera, LOD and difficulty; the **animation panel** is below it; the right pane is **Inspector | Scenario**. Entering never moves the camera, plays or evaluates paths (only the [playback range](#transport) is measured, in the background); an unfinished edit is asked about first.

- **Command bar:** **Exit animation** ([below](#keys-and-exit)), then the opened definition: its name opens a searchable list of the map's definitions, the caption names its file and the binding it plays (choose another with ▾ when it has several), and **‹ ›** go back and forward through the definitions you opened. **Overlay** and **Zones** hide: zone painting waits until you exit. At the right end the **scenario chip** sums up the scenario (`Scenario · 2 starts · 24 calls · 1 forced`, with a dot when it differs from the defaults); it opens the Scenario tab. In a narrow window Camera and Display move to **⋯**.
- **Animation panel:** the [transport](#transport) row first, then the view switch **Structure | Timing | Split** (Split needs a 1,300 DIP wide panel) with the shown view's tools (those a narrow panel has no room for move to **⋯** beside the notices chip; a **⋯** flyout opens with its first control focused, **Tab** stays inside it and **Esc** closes it, back on **⋯**). [Structure](#structure) opens by default, Split in wide windows; [Timing](#timing) shows the run on the shared clock. Drag the gap above the panel to resize it; the project remembers its height.
- **Right pane:** the selection's header (its glyph, name and kind, **Frame** and **✕**), then the [Inspector](#inspector) (the card of what is selected) and the [Scenario](#scenario).
- **World tree** hides in Animation mode until you show it there with its status-bar toggle (remembered for your profile); choosing another definition in it opens that one. **Diagnostics** is a strip under the panel, hidden by default (its toggle is remembered per project); a failure while previewing shows in the transport's status line and never opens anything. Drag the gap above the strip to resize it; it takes at most the room the panel leaves (at least 96 DIP), and the panel keeps its height. MCP `workspace_view` `navigator`, `tools` and `toolsHeight` change these in the mode, never the map layout's panes.
- **Status bar:** the Inspector's selection (`flare_sequence › Player within 64? · condition`, or the opened definition when nothing is selected), then `Animation mode · destroy_tr_bridge`. **Hide UI** (F10) and **Fullscreen** (F11) work as everywhere.
- **Notices:** the view bar's **⚠ n** chip counts the opened animation's notices about its items (prerequisites ignored for a start, refused starts, calls to animations the map does not have, [excluded calls that would change the run](#scenario), preview limits, notes about an event or sequence) and lists them, each with **Go to** (its node in Structure or its lane in Timing; an excluded call also with **Include**), then **n preview approximations ▸** (how the preview approximates the game for these kinds of events: effect rendering, collision, activation order), folded, and **Show in Diagnostics**; with only approximations the chip reads **ⓘ n**. The nodes and lanes a notice concerns carry a **⚠** count, and the item's Inspector card starts with it: a sequence, event or condition card shows only the notices naming it, the definition's card also the folded approximations line.
- **Legend:** **ⓘ** in the view bar shows the edges, Timing's marks, the run states (each as Structure's run chip and Timing's lane glyph draw it, Paused and Stopped included) and the event families.

The first time you enter it, a tip says that the map stays where it is and the scenario is kept; once dismissed it stays dismissed for your profile. It is a deterministic animation preview at 1/60 s ticks with explicit scheduled starts, not a gameplay simulator: it does not simulate hits, combat, AI, the mission's startup or the player tank's driving.

### Transport

**Play/Pause**, **Stop** (the static map at 0 s, with the chosen player tank; the scenario stays), **Go to the range start**, and **step back/forward** one preview tick of 1/60 s. The time box shows the playhead in seconds (`22.217 s`); type a time and press **Enter** to seek, and the preview shows the tick at or before it. **of 0–X s** opens the playback range: why it ends where it does, its start and end, **Extend +5 s**, **Auto** and **Fit range to starts** (the latest start plus 30 s). Then **Speed** (0.25×, 0.5×, 1×, 2×), **Loop range**, sound (mute and volume) and **Follow camera** (off by default; navigating turns it off); in a narrow panel they move to **⋯**. Speed, Loop range, sound and Follow camera also change while an edit is unfinished; a new playback range (and **Extend +5 s**, **Auto** and **Fit range to starts**) asks about the edit first. The status at the right says what the run evaluated (`Evaluated 0–24.0 s · 136 occurrences`), that it is stale, the latest status of the run (path evaluation, sound) or why it failed, with **Show in Diagnostics** (an action refused for an unfinished edit or drop-window move says so until none is left); a long status ends with … and shows whole in its tooltip. Every time in Animation mode is in seconds; the run is bounded to 0–3,600 s.

The playback range is **Auto** unless you choose otherwise. It is measured in the background as soon as you open an animation or another binding of it, again whenever the scenario changes and after an edit; nothing plays, seeks or moves meanwhile, and playback never waits for it. Until it arrives the range reads `of 0–… s` and the flyout `Auto · measuring…`. Auto ends:

- where the run ends, with its sounds and effects, when everything in it simply ends (keyframes, timed motion, timed events);
- where every loop has gone round once, when something loops forever (a sequence whose LOOP has no count; a loop shorter than a second shows for a second). A constant spin counts as such a loop, one revolution its round: an `OBJECT_MOTION` without `RUN_TIME` that only turns its object with `XYZ_ROTATION` (a radar dish, copter rotors, the m4 bridge's tumbling pieces), measured from its rate and any acceleration; one slower than a revolution a minute counts as motion instead;
- **5 s after the last timed event** when something goes on without a timed end: debris falling until it hits the ground (turning as it falls or not), a launch, events that follow a landing (an event that waits for its animation's or sequence's own clock keeps its time: a piece that lands and then changes at 8 s on that clock is timed at 8 s), a binding waiting for the player or the view's zones, a sequence of a running animation waiting for a release that never came (once its animation has ended, nothing can release it); a sound or effect, or a spin's first revolution, that outlasts those 5 s ends the range instead;
- with several starts and included calls, at the latest of these. A range that is not the run's own end lasts at least 5 s.

The flyout says why (`Auto · 5 s after the last timed event (6.100 s) · pieces fall until they hit the ground`, `… · motion has no timed end · sound tail to 5.550 s`, `… · pieces fall until they hit the ground · spins: rotate_piece1 and rotate_piece2 once round to 34.583 s`, `Auto · loops: rotate_it once round`, `Auto · spins: rotate once round`). Whenever the playhead rests at an end the 5 s rule set while the run goes on (playback stopped there, or a seek or step took it there), the status says `Ended 5 s after the last timed event · motion may continue` (`Ended at the last sound or effect tail · …` or `Ended where every spin has gone round once · …` when one of those set it) with **Extend +5 s**, which lengthens the range and plays on from there. **Extend +5 s** in the flyout, or **+5 s** at the range's end on Timing's ruler, only lengthens it. Each lengthens the range by 5 s and makes it **Custom**, as typing a start or end or **Fit range to starts** does; **Auto** brings the measured range back (and measures again if needed). Opening an animation starts on Auto; an edit (**Apply**, **Undo**, **Redo**) ends the run and returns the playhead to 0 s, keeping your Auto or Custom choice, while a plain **Save** keeps the run, the playhead and the range.

### Structure

Structure draws the opened binding as a graph that reads left to right and always looks the same for the same definition:

- **Activation** (left): how the binding starts (when called, on startup, after weapon or collision damage, new game start), its gates, what happens at a stop or reset, its attach node, the animations that call it back, and its **prerequisites**: before a start, each one's state at the playhead; once the binding was started, each group and item as its latest start found them (**met**, **unmet · refused**, or **unmet · ignored for this start** when the scenario's rule let the start run where the game would refuse it). Animations that call it without being called back sit under it.
- **The definition frame** (centre): the **Cleanup** sequence (dashed) and the runtime sequences in source order, filled column by column. A sequence that **waits for release** shows a green pill where its releases arrive. Each event is one row in the animation vocabulary (`Motion piece3 · gravity`, `Call trnsplsh.flt at piece3`, `Release mainsplash`) with its family glyph and threshold badge (`prev + 0.1 s`, `anim ≥ 23 s`); runs of similar events form one row (`Call rdrail_destroy01 … 08 (8)`, its chevron lists them). A sequence shows its first twelve rows, then **Show all n events**; a definition with more than twelve sequences opens its first six. A loop is a bracket from the first event to its **Loop** row; IF … ENDIF is one block with its question, its outcome line and both branches labelled (`if true (nothing)`, `if false`), an ELSEIF with a question and switch of its own.
- **Called animations** (right): one node per called definition and its bindings (`rdrail_destroy0* · 8 bindings`), with its file, how it starts, its own calls and what needs it. **Expand** (▸ or a double-click) shows one frame to its right for the whole group; its header's switcher (`rdrail01 · 1 of 8 ▾`) chooses the binding it shows (the run overlay is that binding's), and a line under it sums up every binding's state. Expanding never includes or runs anything (at most 64 expanded at once). **Open** (▷) opens it in Animation mode (**‹** returns).
- **Edges:** calls (a double line calls and waits, a square at the start is a local child), releases (green; dotted on ground contact), stops, resets and invalidations (rose; dashed when by name: the first binding with that name), prerequisites (dotted violet into the activation). A call back to an animation already on screen is one dashed **back-link** over the top (`↩ calls back (cycle)`). Edges never cross a node. Hover an edge for what it does; click it to select its event.
- **Conditions:** every IF and ELSEIF has a **Game · True · False** switch on its outcome line. Game applies the game's rule with the scenario's inputs; True or False forces it in that call path (the tooltip names it). The outcome line reads `false · game · 20.000 s` or `true · forced`; a condition tested on several passes shows a pip per pass, filled when true, ringed when forced: a few on the outcome line, more (a polling loop always) on a strip of their own under it, the latest 40 and `+n` for the rest; point at a pip for its pass and time, click it to seek there. A switch is a scenario change: playback stops and the run is re-evaluated up to the playhead.
- **Calls are included on their port:** the dot at a call row's right edge is filled when the preview runs that call, hollow when it is excluded; click it to include or exclude it (excluding also excludes what lies below it). A hollow port with **⚠** (and its called animation's node) marks an excluded call that would change the run in the game; its tooltip says how. **Include ▾** includes every call, this animation's calls only (the other starts' calls are excluded), or none. Back-links stay excluded until you include them.
- **The run at the playhead** (Run overlay, on by default): each sequence's state (Running, Complete, Waiting for release with what released it or `no releaser left`, `+0.1 s` waiting for a threshold, Paused while a range or zone gate holds its binding (its clocks frozen), Blocked, whose link selects the call it waits for and its include toggle, Stopped, Not started; one rule decides it for the graph and Timing alike: a sequence of an instance that has ended reads Complete only if it completed, and Stopped if it existed but was still running, waiting or never ran when its instance ended, so the completed sequences of a stopped binding stay Complete; Not started is for one with no evidence at all), the current event with a green bar and `now`, ✓ on executed rows, `stopped` on a row whose event a stop cut short, hatching on rows a condition skipped, faded rows not reached yet, the binding's state on the activation, each called animation's instances, and edges at full strength once they happened. After a Stop, a draft or a scenario change on a stopped run the overlay fades (`Stale · from the last run` or `from before your edit`) and **Re-run to here** runs it again; a source edit rebuilds the map and ends the run.
- **Selecting** a sequence, row or condition shows its card in the Inspector, outlines the objects it names in the view and scrolls Timing to it; selecting an object in the view marks the rows naming it with an amber bar. Selecting or pointing at a range test (a `Player within 64 of rlbridge?` question or a range-gated activation) draws it in the view ([below](#range-tests-and-the-player)).
- **Moving around:** Ctrl+wheel zooms at the pointer (25–200 %), the wheel and Shift+wheel scroll, a middle drag, a drag on empty space or **Space**+drag pans (Space alone still plays), the minimap (bottom right) pans, **Fit** shows everything. Below 75 % rows drop their threshold badges, below 50 % nodes show their headers, below 30 % only names with a large mark for their run state. A new binding opens fitted when that keeps 75 %, else at 100 % from the top left. **Collapse all** / **Expand all** fold the sequences.

### Timing

Timing shows what the run did, on the shared clock in seconds, one lane per sequence. Nothing on it is a schedule: a mark is something the run did, at the time it did it.

- **Ruler:** seconds, with ticks every 1, 0.5 or 0.1 s depending on the zoom; outside the playback range is shaded, and **+5 s** at its end lengthens it (Extend +5 s; it steps beside the playhead's time when they meet, and shows once Auto has measured the end); the playhead is the orange line with its time. Click or drag the ruler to seek; a seek past the visible range, as past the playback range's end, brings the playhead into view. **Ctrl+wheel** zooms at the pointer, **Shift+wheel** pans, the wheel scrolls the lanes; the visible range is independent of the playback range. **Fit range** fits the playback range; its **▾** also offers **Fit run** (everything the run did) and **Fit selection**. Drag the label column's edge to widen it (160–420).
- **Starts lane:** one ▼ per scenario start (the primary ★ in orange). Drag one to move the start (0.025 s steps, **Shift** 0.1 s, **Ctrl** free); right-click it to **Remove start**. Starts at the same time are one marker with a count (`★ destroy_tr_bridge 0.000 s +1`): point at it for every start it holds, drag it to move the one on top (the primary, unless you chose another), click it to choose which start is on top, right-click it to remove one. A yellow dot says the start ran only because the scenario's prerequisite rule ignored its unmet prerequisites; a refused start shows in red with its reason. A click opens the Scenario tab. Moving or removing a start is a scenario change: playback stops and the run is re-evaluated up to the playhead.
- **Instance groups:** each start's binding (`destroy_tr_bridge · rlbridge #241`), when it started and its state (`running · invalidated`, `finished · pose kept`, `stopped`) as a band, then its lanes. The animations its calls reached nest under it, grouped as Structure groups them (`rdrail_destroy0* ×8`); a call back into an animation already above it in that nesting joins that animation's group instead of nesting again (Structure draws it as a back-link). A called group opens with its chevron, and a closed group shows its lanes' marks on its own row.
- **Lanes** carry their state as bands: **waiting for release** (green hatching, with what released it or `no releaser left`), **waiting for threshold** (dots, `anim ≥ 23 s → 43.000 s`), **paused** by a range or zone gate (stripes; the binding's clocks are frozen), **blocked** at an excluded call (rose hatching), **stopped**, and ✓ where the sequence completed. A band still going at the playhead runs up to it (`→ now`); one that ended says so (`– end`), and the last band of an instance that ended (stopped, reset or finished) ends with it, never `→ now`. The glyph at the label's right is the state at the playhead, by the same rule as the graph's run overlay.
- **Marks:** a filled diamond for an executed event, a bar for one that took time (fading with ▸ while it runs; one a stop cut short ends where it was stopped, with a cap, and says what stopped it: `stopped by piece3_hit_water › Stop sequence move_piece3`), ◇ with **T** or **F** for a condition (orange outline when forced), a slashed hollow diamond for events a condition skipped, □ for a call the scenario excludes, a slashed ○ for a refused start (its tooltip gives the reason), ⊘ then hatching for a blocked wait, and a green diamond with a line to the lane it released. A thin dashed line on an animation's group row is where an excluded call would stop, reset or invalidate it in the game (`would be stopped by excluded train_fall_trigger`). The colour is the event's family, as in Structure. Marks closer than 4 DIP share one mark with a count; a long run of them (a polling loop) becomes a line with its count. Point at a mark for its event, time and result; click it to select its event (the Inspector, Structure and the view follow) and seek to it.
- **Thresholds** (view bar, off by default) draw, for each event whose anchor the run reached, a bracket from that anchor (the animation's start, the sequence's start or the previous event's completion) to where its start threshold lets it run (`≥ 0.1 s after previous`); when it ran later, the gap says why where that is known (`waited for release`). With an event selected, its bracket's end is a handle: drag it to propose a new threshold in that event's own clock (0.025 s, **Shift** 0.1 s, **Ctrl** free) through the same draft as the Inspector. Marks never drag.
- **Drop windows lane:** for an analyzed flight ([below](#vtol-drop-windows)), under its flight sequence, each window of every flight cycle the run reached as a bar at its authored start on the flight sequence's clock, numbered; after a change the analysis no longer matches (an event draft or edit, the scenario), the bars stay muted with a dashed edge and the lane says **Stale: analyze the drop windows again.** A click selects the window; drag one to move its start (0.025 s, **Shift** 0.1 s, **Ctrl** free; never its length), then **Apply** or **Revert** in the Inspector.
- **Log** (view bar **Lanes | Log**): the same evidence as a table, newest last: Time · Instance · Sequence · Event · Result (`launched`, `excluded`, `false · game`, `released mainsplash`), with **Copy** (**Ctrl+C**), **Follow latest** and how many occurrences the run retained and discarded (and, on very long runs, how many older sequence state changes it discarded: bands before the oldest kept one may be missing; the transport's status and the notices say so too). Trace limits can drop older observations, so an event missing from Timing may still have run. Selecting a row selects its mark and seeks to it.
- Before anything runs Timing says `Nothing has run yet. Play, or click the ruler to seek; the preview runs the scenario up to that time.` After a Stop or an edit the marks fade and the header says `Stale` until the run is evaluated again.

### Inspector

The Inspector shows the card of the selection; with nothing selected (or the definition frame's header) the opened definition's. Its header sits above the **Inspector | Scenario** tabs. A card's sections fold; a field's label is above or left of it.

- **Definition:** **Bindings** (each bound object, a click selects it, **Frame**; with several bindings, the one Animation mode plays is chosen here), **Activation** (how it starts, then Activation, Health, Reset time, Execution by range, Execution by zone, Priority, Save log and Network log, each optional field with its **Use** check box), **Prerequisites** (each required or "at least n of" group, one name or node path per line, with **✓** or **○** beside each at the playhead and the latest start's outcome), **Calls and callers**, **Drop windows** (only for a VTOL flight, [below](#vtol-drop-windows)) and **Source** (file, line, a read-only excerpt, the maps it affects). Selecting the Activation node shows this card at Activation.
- **Sequence:** its name, **Waits for release** (`ACTIVATION ( ON_CALL )`), a note when the game ignores its START_TIME, what releases or stops it (links to those rows) and its loop, its **Run** at the playhead (state, pass, sequence and animation clocks), and **Source**.
- **Event:** its operands as labelled fields (a vector as X, Y and Z boxes, a name as a text box, a choice as a list), its **Start threshold**, its **Occurrences** in the run (time on the shared clock, time in its own clock, result, **Seek**; the first twelve and `+n more`) and **Source**. A call, release or stop adds **Target**: what it resolves to, whether it waits, its local name, and **Included in preview** for its call paths (excluding one also excludes the paths below it, as it says).
- **Condition** (IF, ELSEIF): its question and operand (radius, weight, effects detail, node and distance), **Preview** with a **Game · True · False** switch for each call path that reaches it and the game's rule in words with the current inputs (`The player (at start, 2,010 from rlbridge) is outside 64: false.`, `Seed 1, draw 3: 0.71 > 0.4: false. In the game this is random (40 %).`), its **Outcomes** per pass, its start threshold and source.
- **Called animation** (a reference node): its file, bindings, how it starts and what it calls, **Open** and **Expand here**, its **Call paths** with their include check boxes, and its **Run**.
- **An object picked in the view:** its card, opening with the events of the opened definition that name it when there are any (`Events naming piece3 in destroy_tr_bridge`; each selects its row) and ending with **Animations affecting this object**.
- **A start on Timing's Starts lane** opens the Scenario tab.

**Start threshold:** the clock (**Animation | Sequence | After previous**: choosing another is an edit), the value in seconds with **−** / **+** (0.025 s, **Shift** 0.1 s) and a ruler whose diamond drags (0.025 s, **Shift** 0.1 s, **Ctrl** free), then what it means (`May start 0.1 s after the previous event completes. Earlier events, waits and conditions can delay it.`) and, once the run reached it, when it ran. An event without START_TIME reads **None (animation clock 0)** with **Add threshold**; **Clear** removes it. Negative or off-grid stored values stay exactly as they are when only viewed. Timing's threshold handle and **F2** reach the same field. **Enter** keeps a typed value in the draft; **Esc** restores the box and takes the typing back out of the draft. At a narrow pane the clock's labels wrap rather than shorten.

**Editing** works on existing fields: the first change starts the draft, stops the run (its overlay goes stale) and disables **Play** until you **Apply** (one project undo step; it validates every map that includes the file first) or **Revert** at the card's foot. Source comments and layout stay intact. A shared definition changes every expansion and every map that includes the file. **Save** writes source files (a playing run, the playhead and the selection stay), and only **Export** builds game archives.

### Scenario

The Scenario tab holds the map's one scenario; the chip, Timing's Starts lane and MCP show the same object, so a change anywhere shows everywhere at once. **A change stops playback and re-evaluates the run up to the playhead** (silently, as a seek does), so its effect shows at once. The scenario is saved with the map in the project's hidden `.zstudio\settings.json` and survives Exit, other definitions, difficulty changes and source edits (Apply, undo, redo: starts, call paths and switches follow their source positions). What an edit takes away is kept, marked, at the top of the tab (`1 override no longer matches after your edit` with **Remove** and **Keep**, and a row in Problems): a marked item never runs, is saved with the scenario and comes back when an undo, a redo or a later edit makes it match again. Each marked item names its start or the call it sits in and the sequence (`Included: rdrail_destroy07 at rdrail07 · raildam.zad, called by sequence healthy_to_destroyed of …`). **Keep** stops asking until more of its kind are marked (also after the map is reopened), **Remove** drops it for good; excluding a call path, **Exclude all** or **Include ▾ → This animation only** also drops the marked call paths below it. When an edit takes away the binding of the opened animation itself (also an edit made while the map was closed: opening the animation again does the same), its other binding plays in its place, at the same time, until an undo or the original files bring it back. What you change meanwhile carries over to the original without a mark or a row in Problems: it comes back at the stand-in's time with the call paths and switches you set (a call you exclude stays excluded), and what cannot apply to it is dropped; after **Remove**, **Reset…** or removing the start that played in its place, it does not come back as a marked or extra start (only a second edit that also takes away the binding that played in its place brings it back, marked). Only **Reset…** clears the scenario, marked items included.

- **Starts:** up to 32 bindings on the shared clock. The opened binding is the primary start (★); opening another definition keeps the previous primary as an ordinary start at its time, while choosing another binding of the same definition (the binding picker) replaces the primary at its time. Type each start's seconds; **✕** removes a start; **+ Add start…** searches every binding of the map; **Fit range to starts** sets the playback range (Custom).
- **Called animations:** the calls the starts reach, grouped by calling and called definition, each with `n of m` included; none is included until you include it. **Include all** includes every reachable call except back-links (a call back into its own chain, which the game refuses while that animation runs) and says what it did; **Exclude all** clears them. A group's box includes or excludes all its call paths; expand it for each path. Including a path includes the calls that lead to it; excluding one excludes everything below it. An included call runs when the script reaches it; it is not started by the inclusion. An excluded call that its sequence waits for blocks that sequence; an included call to an animation the map does not have is reported and skipped. An excluded call that would change the run in the game is flagged: the preview checks it in the background by running the same scenario with that call and everything below it included, to the end of the playback range, and when that run stops, resets or invalidates an animation of the run (a start, or one the run started), a notice says so on the call's row and on that animation with the time it happened (`train_fall_trigger is excluded: in the game it stops destroy_tr_bridge at 23.017 s`); **Include** includes exactly what that check ran and runs it again; Structure marks its port and called animation with ⚠, and Timing draws a dashed line at that time. The check takes the scenario as it is: its inputs, rules and condition switches decide the conditions, prerequisites and range gates on the way, so a call the game would refuse, a branch not taken, an animation that stops itself first or a stop the run makes anyway at that moment flags nothing. While the checks run, the notices chip reads **n · checking…**; the first 8 such calls of a run are checked, and the chip says how many more were not.
- **Conditions:** **Player** is where range conditions and range-gated animations measure from: **At start** (the mission's first AIV spawn; callbacks 10 and 17 move it, 11 freezes it, as in the game; m2–m6 start from the drop-off without their opening), **Custom** (**Pick in view**: click a surface; right-click or **Esc** cancels) or **No player** (range conditions are false). **Random seed** fixes the preview's draws (in the game they are random). **Effects detail** is the game option ANIMATION_LOD conditions test (High with Direct3D). **Overrides** lists the conditions forced true or false, IF and ELSEIF alike, with **Go to** and **✕**.
- **Rules:** **Prerequisites** *Check* (as the game: a start with unmet prerequisites is refused), *Ignore for starts* (default: your scheduled starts run, everything the run starts is checked) or *Ignore*; **Range gates** *As the game* (default) or *Ignore*; **Zone gates** *Use the view's zones* or *Ignore* (default). Each counts what it affects in the current run (Prerequisites: the starts it refused and those that ran with unmet prerequisites).
- **World:** **Phase** (Runtime, or the Cleanup sequence alone), preview-only **Height**, and **Beam origin** (where beams that take the activation's position start; the root by default).

For M4, open `destroy_tr_bridge`, add `fill_canal` as a start at 0 seconds, set the bridge to 20 seconds (or 35 for the filled canal) and **Include all**: the bridge collapses while the water rises. `mainsplash` waits for release until `move_piece3` releases it; splash sequences wait for contact.

### Paths

The 3D view's **Paths ▾** chip (after **Perspective**, in Animation mode) says what the opened binding's motion guides are: **not evaluated**, **shown**, **hidden**, **out of date** (the scenario or the binding changed) or **partial**, with a ring while they evaluate. Its flyout:

- **Evaluate paths** computes the guides for the opened binding with the scenario and its included call paths, silently: the playhead, playback, audio, camera and panel stay as they are. Its progress and any failure show on the chip and in the transport's status line.
- **Show guides** shows or hides a matching evaluated result (it never evaluates); **Frame paths** frames them; **Clear cached paths** discards them.
- **Contacts and impacts** marks where moving objects touched the ground (⊥) and where a contact played its impact sound (◎).
- **Show range tests** draws every range test of the opened animation and its expanded calls ([below](#range-tests-and-the-player)).
- The legend and the result: `Evaluated for this scenario · 6 tracks · 1,904 samples · 0–30 s`, or why it is out of date.

Object paths are teal and camera paths orange. Crosses mark endpoints, arrows show direction and discontinuous transforms break the line. Guides carry time: a dot on every guide at the playhead and faint ticks every second; selecting a motion event emphasises its guide. A path represents one bounded scenario; changes to the source, the binding, the map's starting scene (its difficulty), the phase, height or inclusion make it out of date, and partial results show their coverage. Exit hides the guides; their result stays for **Show guides**. Analysis is limited to 128 tracks, 216,001 samples and 15 seconds; up to 32 compatible result sets and 432,002 aggregate points are retained. Dependency inspection pages at most 100 references and retains at most 4,096 call paths; excess work is refused explicitly. The run has separate limits, including 256 simultaneous instances and effects and 10,000 dispatches per tick.

Paths and playback share the same scenario and collision. Gravity motion lands on the active standable surfaces beneath each moving node's origin, excluding the node's own bound assembly: animated surface transforms, active states and morphs take part, so rising water changes when contact happens and hidden healthy geometry stops colliding. Hiding objects in the view does not change collision. A contact releases waiting sequences and plays impact sounds; later scripted sinking remains possible. Procedural movement stops at world Y −500 or 500, including Height, without an impact; keyframes and cameras are unrestricted.

### Range tests and the player

A range condition (`Player within 64 of rlbridge?`) and a range gate (**Execution by range**) measure from the scenario's **Player** to the binding's attach node. With one selected or under the pointer in Structure, the view draws a green ring of its radius around the attach node, the player as a pin and a dashed line between them labelled with the distance and the outcome (`2,010 · outside 64 → false`; a gate reads `→ runs` or `→ paused`). When the player is off screen an arrow at the view's edge points to it with its distance. **Show range tests** draws every range test of the opened animation and its expanded calls at once. The ring sits where the run has the attach node at the playhead, also when it is a node without a model that moves with its animation (a VTOL's drop point), else where its latest test measured from, else where the map places it. The player is where the run has it at the playhead: at start, moved by callback 10 or 17 and frozen by callback 11 as in the game, or the scenario's custom point.

### VTOL drop windows

A recognised VTOL flight (a flight whose drop schedule calls a callback-911 program) offers **Drop windows** in Animation mode's Inspector, on its Definition card. **Analyze drop windows** runs a separate analysis that admits only the flight's recognised callsites, listed there (`Admits 5 callsites: call_airdrops › Call call_pchute at 9.000 s, …`); it leaves the main run's inclusion and playback unchanged. **Drop analysis follows the scenario's condition switches**, its player and its rules: a release gated by the player's range happens only while the scenario's player is in range, as in the game, unless the switch on that condition forces it. Changing the scenario's player, switches or rules makes a shown analysis stale; analyze again for the new scenario. Limited or incomplete analysis gives diagnostics without editable windows.

The result is one complete flight cycle: the flight path, a cyan start marker per window and orange release markers where cargo is released (not where it lands), and the window list (window, flight sequence start, duration). Timing shows the windows on their own lane under the flight sequence.

To move a window, select it (in the list, at its marker in the view or on Timing's lane), then use **Move along path** (the banner `Moving drop window 2 · click a point on the flight path · right-click or Esc cancels`; a click places the start there), type its start in **Start (s)**, or drag its bar on Timing's lane. Review the validated release markers and choose **Apply** (one project undo step) or **Revert**. The move changes exactly the CALL's SEQUENCE_OFFSET start; it keeps the paired STOP's EVENT_OFFSET duration and the other windows, and there is no resize. A definition expanded onto several bindings can't be moved this way. Exit, another map or a rebuild ends a move in progress, and Exit closes the analysis.

The Inspector's event fields still edit a recognised CALL or STOP through the same source draft; that can change or remove its drop-window relationship, which only the move above keeps intact. Beginning an event draft makes the analysis stale; analyze again after editing.

A compiled Whole world shows the same analysis read only ([Compiled game files](compiled-files.md#whole-world)).

## Zones

The **Zones** section lists the zones the map uses, each with its colour, label and polygon count. Show a zone's colour with its eye (all start hidden); the header's **Objects | Polygons** switch colours objects by their zone or polygons by their zone sets. Select a zone to edit its **Label** (saved in the map's master model), see what uses it, or **Paint with this zone**.

The **Edit zones** toggle in the Zones group (or **Tools → Paint polygon zones** / **Erase polygon zones**, **P** and **E**) starts the polygon zone tool:
- Drag over the map to paint the chosen zone on every polygon you cross, or erase it; each stroke is one undo step.
- **Ctrl** inverts the mode for a stroke, **Shift**+click picks a zone from the polygon under the pointer, **[** and **]** step through the map's zones, **Escape** drops a stroke, then leaves the tool.
- Paint adds the zone after a polygon's others (a polygon keeps at most three); painting Any replaces them, and a numbered zone replaces Any, as in the shipped maps, where a polygon that lists Any lists nothing else. Erasing the last zone leaves none, which differs from Any.
- Only the map's own models (`data\mN\models`) take zones; shared models, vehicles, units and pickups don't. A placement takes its own zones; when they come to differ from the other placements of the same model, the build gives that placement a model of its own and the world is rebuilt in full.
- A stroke covers at most 4,096 polygons on 1,024 placements.

MCP: `zstudio_source_zones`, `zstudio_source_polygon_zones`, `zstudio_source_zone_tool`, `zstudio_source_zone_label`.

## Terrain

Terrain is authored as whole surfaces in glTF plus a recipe beside them (`name.terrain.json`) that holds the gameplay attributes painted on them; the build cuts the surfaces into the pieces the engine needs.
- **Tools → Create terrain…** takes a glTF file of its own and the meshes in it that are terrain, writes the recipe and adds a terrain marker to the mission database.
- Select a terrain piece and choose **Edit terrain…** on its card: the recipe opens in the Properties window, with its defaults, regions (painted areas, in the order they apply) and the brush: **Paint in viewport** or **Erase in viewport**, then drag over the terrain, or **Paint polygon** / **Erase polygon** with typed corners for exact edges. Each stroke is one undo step.
- Attributes: zones, node zone, altitude gate, collision, standable, craters (allowed, blocked or ignored), soil (default, water, seafloor, quicksand, lava, fire), priority and flags.
- Stacked sheets, such as a cave floor under its ceiling, must be separate surfaces: the engine's ground probe takes the first polygon of a node.
- **Tools → Convert to editable terrain…** turns a shipped map's terrain pieces into surfaces and recipes. It lists what it keeps as objects (landmarks, transformed or named pieces, parts) before changing anything, and compares the ground heights before and after. The original piece layout and names are lost; **Undo** takes the conversion back.

MCP: `zstudio_source_terrain`, `zstudio_source_terrain_create`, `zstudio_source_terrain_edit`, `zstudio_source_terrain_convert`.

## Models from other missions

Adding a model to a world is available through MCP only: `zstudio_source_world_add_model` loads any glTF model of the project, such as a vehicle only another mission uses. Not placed, it loads as a template that resources place copies of by name (`ltank_01` places a copy of `ltank`); placed, it goes at a position and heading. It can also add the animation definitions other missions list for it. The edit adds the script lines the shipped scripts use, before the line that writes the world:

```
SetModelDirectory ..\data\common\models
LoadGameGen ltank.flt ltank
```

An addition is refused when a folder the mission searches first holds another file of the same name; rename the model.

`zstudio_source_map_parts` and `zstudio_source_map_parts_visibility` hide or show parts of a placed reference in the view only.

## Undo and save

**Undo**, **Redo** and **Save** in the command bar (**Ctrl+Z**, **Ctrl+Y**, **Ctrl+S**) apply to the whole project ([Save and recover](source-project.md#save-and-recover)). Simple moves update the view in place; larger edits rebuild the world (a card over the view shows **Rebuilding the world…**), keeping the camera and open World tree rows. When a file changes outside zStudio, the map asks to reload before further edits. **Tools → Validate source file on disk** evaluates the map from the saved files, without your unsaved edits.

## Limits

- AI links, new groups and Add model can't be edited in the GUI; positions that startup animations set can't be moved, nor a unit that two records of the shown difficulty place (m2 Easy lists the player tank twice; its AI Vehicles rows say so in their tooltips) or a unit hidden at the mission's start. The player start can't be moved where the mission's opening holds the controls and releases them from the tank's pose (m1–m6): in m2–m6 the opening also sets where the player starts; in m1 the start is the placed pose. It moves in m7–m13.
- Deleting or copying an object an animation names, or changing the model of an object whose parts a bound animation names, isn't refused: the rebuild and the export report it in Problems.
- The view is the mission's starting scene, without simulating gameplay; not every kind of change has been tested in the game.
- Animations edit existing fields only: events can't be inserted, deleted or reordered, and keyframe shapes can't be changed.
- The AI overlay draws at most 16,384 nodes and 32,768 links.
- Terrain recipes have no tilted region planes for walls and no texture page cutting.
